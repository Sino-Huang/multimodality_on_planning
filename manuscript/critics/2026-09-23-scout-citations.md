## Papers surveyed

The items below give the venue or preprint year, method, evaluated data, reported result, and distinction from this paper's contract-level measurement question. Each linked source was fetched with `read`.

### Evaluation validity and benchmark coverage

- **PlanBench — arXiv preprint, 2022; existing key `valmeekam2023planbench`.** It is an extensible benchmark based on automated-planning domains, including domains used in the International Planning Competition. Its experiments cover plan generation and other planning capabilities, and the abstract reports that LLM performance falls short on critical capabilities. This benchmarks plans and plan-relevant judgments, while this paper tests whether an execution interface exposes any choice signal. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10498).
- **BEHAVIOR-1K — arXiv preprint, 2024; added as `li2024behavior1k`.** The paper defines 1,000 everyday activities in 50 scenes with more than 9,000 annotated objects and provides the OmniGibson simulator. Its experiments characterize the activities as long-horizon and challenging for current robot-learning systems, with an initial simulation-to-real transfer study. It evaluates embodied task execution rather than identity between a random menu policy and an exact search reference. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09227).
- **GSM-Symbolic — arXiv preprint, 2024; screened but not added.** The authors generate controlled variants of GSM8K questions from symbolic templates. They report performance drops under changed numerical values and up to a 65% drop after adding an irrelevant clause. This is a useful reasoning-benchmark perturbation study, but PlanBench already supports the compact planning/reasoning-validity point in this paragraph. I did not add a second benchmark citation. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229).

### Multimodal cues and process supervision

- **Zhu et al., IJCAI 2020; added as `zhu2020overcoming`.** Their self-supervised VQA method generates balanced data and uses an auxiliary task to reduce language-prior reliance. On VQA-CP v2, the proceedings page reports accuracy improving from 49.50 to 57.59. It studies visual grounding in answer prediction, not state grounding by a search-operation policy. [Proceedings source](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/2020/151).
- **Loginova et al., ACL 2025; added as `loginova2025addressing`.** The paper diagnoses selection bias in three video-language models using video MCQA datasets NExT-QA, its NExT-GQA subset, STAR, Perception Test, and Video-MME, with eleven dataset modifications. The authors find that position preferences persist across accuracy-relevant settings and that their BOLD calibration reduces bias metrics while generally improving performance for Video-LLaMA and Video-LLaVA. This is answer-option position bias, not a search-frontier or menu-identity test. [ACL Anthology source](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.162/).
- **Balasubramanian et al., Findings of EMNLP 2025; added as `balasubramanian2025closer`.** They evaluate bias articulation and chain-of-thought faithfulness in LVLMs, using 100 CV-Bench depth questions plus Waterbirds and CelebA, and report that subtle visual cues are less often articulated than explicit textual cues. Their outputs are free-form reasoning traces, unlike this paper's typed runtime-validated operations and menu manipulations. [ACL Anthology source](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.723/).
- **Lightman et al., ICLR 2024; existing key `lightman2023lets`.** The paper compares outcome supervision with per-step process supervision on MATH and releases PRM800K, a dataset of 800,000 step-level human feedback labels. It reports that process supervision outperforms outcome supervision and solves 78% of a representative MATH test subset. Its unit is reasoning-step feedback, not a grounded planning selector under a runtime contract. [ICLR proceedings source](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/aca97732e30bcf1303bc22ac3924fd16-Abstract-Conference.html).

### Learned heuristics and search selection

- **Orseau et al., NeurIPS 2018; added as `orseau2018singleagent`.** Single-Agent Policy Tree Search introduces best-first policy-guided enumeration and a sampling variant with node-expansion bounds. The empirical task is 1,000 generated Sokoban levels using an A3C-trained policy, and the paper reports competitiveness with a domain-independent heuristic planner. It selects within symbolic search trees and does not test a multimodal grounded-menu contract. [NeurIPS source](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2018/hash/52c5189391854c93e8a0e1326e56c14f-Abstract.html).
- **Orseau and Lelis, AAAI 2021; added as `orseau2021policyguided`.** Policy-guided Heuristic Search combines a learned policy and heuristic with search-loss guarantees. It evaluates sliding-tile puzzles, Sokoban, and The Witness, comparing A*, weighted A*, greedy best-first, LevinTS, and PUCT by problems solved and search time. This directly motivates named algorithmic selectors as stronger calibration references than a random floor, while its policy and heuristic operate within a symbolic search algorithm. [AAAI source](https://aaai.org/papers/12382-policy-guided-heuristic-search-with-guarantees/).
- **Chrestien et al., NeurIPS 2023; added as `chrestien2023optimize`.** This work learns heuristic functions with ranking losses for forward search rather than regressing cost-to-go. It evaluates eight domains, including grid and PDDL tasks such as Sokoban, mazes, sliding puzzles, Blocksworld, Ferry, and Elevators. The paper reports solved-task fractions and expanded-state statistics, with ranking losses outperforming regression losses in its comparisons. Its learned object is a heuristic trained from solved plans, not a vision-language model selecting among runtime-owned frontier states. [NeurIPS proceedings source](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/50ea4dbd1cff6bd3daef939eff10c092-Abstract-Conference.html) and [paper PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/50ea4dbd1cff6bd3daef939eff10c092-Paper-Conference.pdf).
- **Yonetani et al., ICML 2021; added as `yonetani2021path`.** Neural A* couples a convolutional encoder to a differentiable A* module trained from expert paths. It evaluates Motion Planning, tiled Motion Planning, City/Street Map, and Stanford Drone Dataset inputs, reporting improved path-optimality/search-efficiency tradeoffs over data-driven baselines and improved pedestrian-trajectory similarity. Its encoder guides A* priorities rather than issuing typed operations to choose a frontier state. [PMLR proceedings source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/yonetani21a.html).
- **Tamar et al., NeurIPS 2016; verified but not added.** Value Iteration Networks embed a differentiable approximation of value iteration in a neural network and evaluate discrete and continuous path planning plus a natural-language search task, reporting generalization to unseen domains. This is embedded planning computation rather than a frontier-selector comparison, so it was not needed in the scoped paragraph. [NeurIPS source](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/c21002f464c5fc5bee3b98ced83963b8-Abstract.html).
- **Lehnert et al., arXiv preprint, 2024; existing key `lehnert2024beyond`.** Searchformer predicts A* search dynamics and is fine-tuned by expert iterations. The arXiv abstract reports 93.7% success on previously unseen Sokoban puzzles and up to 26.8% fewer search steps than its A* training implementation. The task is symbolic search-trace generation, not menu-grounded state choice with a runtime audit. [Primary arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14083). An official ICLR 2024 workshop poster page also exists and is noted below because its title, author list, and abstract results differ.
