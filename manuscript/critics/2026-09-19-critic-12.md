## Findings

- **MAJOR — the opening conclusion is materially broader than the evidence it subsequently admits.** “**Process supervision can teach vision-language models executable structural search**” (`iclr2026_conference.tex:57`) reads as a general capability claim. The supporting positive result is one fixed-seed, text-state BFWS run over 15 selected development tasks; it has neither held-out confirmation nor a second-backbone outcome (`docs/issue-59-bfws-structural-gate.md:258-261`; `second-backbone-valid-stop.md:35-44`). Worse, the expanded matched baseline has process SFT fail all BFS and width-based cells in every modality (`baseline-progress.md:62-66`). The final caveat limits efficacy, but it does not fully undo the headline's broad “can teach VLMs” formulation. State the existential result at its actual boundary—one BFWS development comparison under the declared interface—rather than lead with an unqualified cross-model, cross-setting conclusion.

- **MAJOR — “algorithm- and headroom-dependent” over-interprets a confounded descriptive pattern.** The Abstract says the 100% random-valid ceilings “**suggest[] learned advantage depends on control headroom**” and concludes it “**remains algorithm- and headroom-dependent**” (`iclr2026_conference.tex:57`); the Introduction repeats “**headroom-dependent map**” (`:78,86,90`). Saturation establishes only that advantage cannot be *measured* in those cells. It does not show that the underlying learned advantage depends on headroom. Likewise, algorithm labels are entangled with different frozen panels, task distributions, and control behavior: the same width-based family is positive on the selected 15-task BFWS panel but `0/24` in every modality in the expanded baseline (`issue-59-bfws-structural-gate.md:250-261`; `baseline-progress.md:62-66`). Call this an observed, panel-specific pattern of advantage and saturation, not a demonstrated dependence.

- **MINOR — the 220-word Abstract is evidence-dense to the point that its main result is hard to retain.** The single paragraph packs the interface, four BFWS values, a confidence bound, three saturated panels, a 288-episode baseline, two negative extensions, and five scope exclusions (`iclr2026_conference.tex:57`). It remains grammatical and factually coherent, but the reader must re-read it to recover the hierarchy: one positive BFWS development signal, then several boundaries. Trim repeated qualification rather than dropping a limitation.

## Verdict

**WEAK REJECT.** This is an unusually candid and largely accurate deadline Abstract, and title, Abstract, and Introduction are synchronized. The single most damaging problem is converting one development-panel BFWS signal into the unqualified opening claim that process supervision can teach VLMs executable structural search.
