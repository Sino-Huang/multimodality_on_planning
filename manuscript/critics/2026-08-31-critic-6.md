# Critique — 2026-08-31

## Scope

Reviewed only the written title, temporary abstract, and Introduction in `manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex:12,55-81`. Per the retained evidence boundary, the BFS-only text-state corpus/release qualification is not a trained-policy or efficacy result.

## Findings

- **CRITICAL — the paper's central attribution is unearned: it calls runtime-mediated behavior “model-owned” algorithm execution without defining what the model actually controls.** The abstract claims that the separation “**enables model-owned exploration decisions while retaining deterministic control over search bookkeeping**” (`manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex:56`), and the Introduction escalates this to “**we can measure structural/process competence directly**” because the runtime holds bookkeeping (`:67`). But the only operational description says the runtime “**applies these operations, maintains algorithm state, and checks Algorithm Invariants**” (`:65`) while the policy emits labels such as “**expand, generate successor, update frontier, or goal test**” (`:65`). Nothing written establishes which operands, node choices, tie-breaking choices, successor choices, or frontier order are supplied by the policy rather than selected or completed by the runtime. A deterministic runtime that selects any of these can itself implement the claimed search behavior while a policy merely emits admissible commands. Consequently, invariant compliance and episode success cannot identify *model-owned* exploration from the stated interface. This is not a missing-detail quibble; it invalidates the paper's proposed distinction from external classical search. The manuscript must eventually state an operation schema and ownership boundary sufficient to show that replaying policy emissions, rather than runtime defaults, determines the explored trace; otherwise the headline question is answered about a runtime, not a VLM.

- **CRITICAL — “correctness can be checked at every step” conflates local invariant validity with algorithmic correctness, contradicting the paper's own validity distinction.** “**BFS, iterative widening (IW), and A* are deterministic procedures whose correctness can be checked at every step**” (`:61`) is far stronger than the subsequent mechanism, which checks that an operation satisfies “**the declared contract or an algorithm invariant**” (`:65`). The paper itself correctly says “**validity is not by itself episode success**” (`:65`). Local legality/invariant checks do not, merely by occurring at every step, establish termination, completeness, solution optimality, or even that all necessary choices were made according to the declared algorithm; for A* these properties additionally depend on the stated heuristic and tie-breaking assumptions. The opening therefore uses “correctness” to mean something the later text explicitly refuses to credit as success. This breaks the logical foundation for the operational-versus-structural distinction.

- **MAJOR — the Introduction misnames IW, making the claimed algorithmic scope technically unreliable.** It introduces “**iterative widening (IW)**” (`:61`) but later characterizes the relevant invariant as “**IW novelty pruning**” (`:65`). Novelty pruning is the defining mechanism of *iterated width* (also abbreviated IW), not an explanation of iterative widening. The manuscript's own governed glossary uses “IW novelty pruning,” so this is not harmless prose variation: it leaves the reader unable to know whether the proposed study concerns an iterated-width planner or a widening procedure. The promised BFS/IW/A* breadth is meaningless until the algorithm is named consistently.

- **MAJOR — the abstract retains the two exact claim-discipline failures identified by critic-5, despite calling itself temporary.** “**It reports budgeted episode success separately**” and “**Matched text-state, visual-state, and multimodal-state conditions preserve task semantics and the search-memory interface**” (`:56`) are present-tense statements of completed evaluation controls. The same paragraph only later says “**Comparative experiments are ongoing**” (`:56`), while the Introduction admits the protocol is “**an unfrozen plan rather than a registered protocol**” (`:67`) and that no multimodal corpus or modality matrix exists (`:69`). “Designed to distinguish” does not rescue “reports” or “preserve.” These sentences continue to advertise an implemented, valid comparison that the Introduction expressly says does not exist. This is precisely the unresolved abstract issue from `2026-08-31-critic-5.md`, not a new experimental demand.

- **MAJOR — the related-work gap is an uncited, universal strawman, not academic framing.** The entire positioning paragraph consists of assertions followed by “**[TODO: cite]**” four times (`:63`), culminating in “**The common weakness is that the model's contribution to exploration is neither typed nor mechanically checked, so local competence and full-episode algorithm execution are conflated**” (`:63`). This asserts a broad negative fact about classical planning, neural algorithm execution, LLM/VLM planning, and runtime-constrained execution with zero evidence and no scoped exceptions. Worse, it is the sole written justification for novelty, yet its conclusion is already undermined by the undefined model/runtime ownership boundary above. A top-venue reader cannot assess whether this is a new problem, a terminology relabeling of tool-mediated search, or a false characterization of existing work. Literal citation placeholders make the submitted prose academically nonfunctional.

- **MAJOR — the paper advertises a causal modality comparison while conceding neither the intervention nor the comparison protocol exists.** “**allowing modality effects to be assessed independently of task content**” (`:56`) and “**we also plan to study how the observation modality interacts with this execution**” (`:69`) imply an identifiable modality effect. Yet the conditions are merely “**intended to represent the same state and goal**” and are “**not yet implemented facts**” (`:69`); moreover, the Introduction explicitly leaves split procedure, estimands, and uncertainty rules unset (`:67`). Semantic parity and a fixed memory interface alone do not define what quantity is being attributed to modality, particularly when rendering, tokenization, visual resolution, and training-data allocation can vary. This is causal language without a causal design.

- **MINOR — contribution three is governance boilerplate presented as a scientific contribution, weakening an already evidence-free pitch.** “**A receipt-first evidence discipline after gates run**” (`:76`) promises only that the authors “**do not treat local competence, corpus availability, or release qualification as a scientific outcome**” (`:76`). That is commendable claim hygiene, not a contribution to ML or planning. With the preceding sentence admitting the three items are “**formulation and protocol contributions, not efficacy results**” (`:71`), this bullet makes the manuscript read as a project-management registration rather than an exciting research paper. The “so what?” remains unanswered: why should a venue accept a no-results paper whose third contribution is a promise not to overclaim later?

- **MINOR — the abstract's comparator is still too vague to communicate the planned intervention.** “**interactive data collection**” (`:56`) is listed alongside “base model, operational-only supervision, [and] offline process supervision,” but it does not say whether the comparator is DAgger, human-in-the-loop collection, online updating, or something else. This obscures the only purported causal training contrast in the abstract and conflicts with the Introduction's otherwise careful admission that “**the arm-level data sources**” are not set (`:67`).

## Verdict

**REJECT.** The single most damaging issue is the unsupported attribution of search execution to the VLM when the described Trusted Search Runtime may perform the very exploration and bookkeeping choices used to claim structural/process competence (`manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex:56,65,67`).
