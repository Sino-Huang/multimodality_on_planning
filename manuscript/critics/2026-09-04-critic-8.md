# Critique — 2026-09-04 (motivation and continuity review)

## Scope

Reviewed only the title, abstract, and Introduction in `manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex:13,56-88`, with `content_brief.md`, the manuscript-local context, and critic-7 as context. This review assesses the revised motivation and its stated continuity with arXiv:2607.11197; it does not review unwritten sections.

## What improved

The opening now gives symbolic planning a defensible methodological role rather than treating it as a proxy for general intelligence: “**formal specifications and mechanically verifiable candidate answers**” and systematically variable state-space difficulty make it a controlled reasoning probe (`:62`). Crucially, the final sentence at `:78` keeps transfer to planning benchmarks and broader reasoning explicitly open.

The prior-work bridge is substantially more disciplined. The text names operational reasoning and structural enumeration, calls the evidence “**item-level and text-only**,” and limits it to models “**used as released, without task-specific training**” (`:64`). It also avoids the earlier conflation: “**does not amount to complete search capability**” and “**does not require controlling a frontier across a budgeted episode**” (`:66`) make the distinction from full search explicit. The next move—test training and full budgeted episodes because the predecessor tested neither—is logically intelligible (`:66-68`). The ownership contract, then the bounded BFS result, then the pending BFWS stage and prospective transfer question form a generally coherent academic arc (`:72-88`).

## Findings

- **CRITICAL — the revised motivation identifies a sensible hypothesis, but the written evidence still does not answer the title’s question or support the claimed training intervention.** The central transition promises to test whether training yields “**model-attributable structural/process competence beyond valid but uninformed exploration**” (`:66`). Yet the only completed model result says “**random-valid also solved every selected task**,” with “**the absolute gain and paired-bootstrap lower bound over the best control ... both zero**” (`:74`); the abstract likewise says the learned policy showed “**no gain over the strongest control**” (`:57`). The manuscript correctly labels this a `VALID_STOP`, but that honesty does not repair the contribution: matching random-valid on a 15-task development panel cannot establish that process SFT learned useful search control rather than that the panel makes valid exploration sufficient. BFWS is the only route described toward a more discriminative structural test, but “**training and adjudication remain incomplete**” (`:57,76`). Thus the motivation is clearer than before while the empirical paper remains a stopped feasibility pilot, not an answer to “Can ... Learn.”

- **MAJOR — continuity with arXiv:2607.11197 is asserted but not auditable because the sole citation is a literal placeholder.** “**This paper extends a prior observational study of latent planning competence in off-the-shelf models \emph{[TODO: cite prior work]}**” (`:64`) is the only attribution for every subsequent characterization: the operational/structural distinction, the flat structural profile, and the effects of scaling, prompting, scratchpads, and agent harnesses. The carefully limited scope (“**item-level and text-only**” and released models) is the right scope if it faithfully reports the prior paper, but a reviewer cannot verify that it does. This is not cosmetic bibliography cleanup: the claimed observational finding is the premise for the training-based intervention. Replace the placeholder with the anonymized arXiv:2607.11197 entry and ensure its exact task granularity, modality, model condition, and measured quantities support each sentence in `:64`.

- **MAJOR — “Gains in one did not produce gains in the other” overstates what the manuscript itself calls an observational, item-level result.** The sentence “**Gains in one did not produce gains in the other**” (`:64`) reads as a causal or intervention-level conclusion: improving operational reasoning failed to cause improvement in structural enumeration. But the paragraph immediately characterizes the predecessor as “**observational**” and item-level (`:64`), while the listed manipulations are heterogeneous prompting and harness conditions rather than an identified intervention on one latent competence. The later, defensible descriptive claim—“**structural enumeration remained comparatively flat**” under those conditions (`:64`)—does not license the stronger causal wording. State the observed dissociation/absence of co-improvement, not that gains in one *did not produce* gains in the other, unless the prior study actually performed an appropriately controlled intervention.

- **MINOR — the first paragraph slightly overreaches from a controlled probe to “reasoning abilities,” although the paper later retreats appropriately.** “**Symbolic planning provides a controlled probe of the reasoning abilities of large models**” (`:62`) is broad enough to invite the objection that success on formal planning measures general reasoning. The later statement that transfer to “**broader reasoning, remains an open question**” (`:78`) is the correct limitation, but it arrives sixteen lines later. Qualify `:62` as a probe of reasoning relevant to formally specified state-space tasks, or immediately state that it is not itself a measure of general reasoning. This would make the opening and the prospective-transfer boundary fully consistent rather than merely reconcilable on a close read.

## Verdict / next recommendation

**REJECT.** The revised motivation is now coherent and much better scoped, but its single most damaging issue remains the zero-gain result against random-valid: the paper has no completed evidence that training produces a search capability beyond uninformed valid exploration. Do not strengthen the headline claim; first complete a discriminative, governed BFWS evaluation that can separate trained search control from random-valid exploration. Also resolve the prior-paper citation and causal wording before treating the motivation as publication-ready.
