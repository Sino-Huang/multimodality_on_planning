# Critique — 2026-08-31 (post-audit)

## Scope

Reviewed only the title, abstract, and Introduction in `manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex:12,55-81`, against the current audited BFS/BFWS boundary. The historical claims in `supervisor_brief.md` were treated as superseded, as its status notice requires.

## What is now fixed

Critic-6's two central logical defects are genuinely addressed in the written prose. “**every operand that changes exploration**,” the prohibitions on runtime defaults/reordering, and the raw-emission replay condition (`:65`) are a credible, falsifiable ownership boundary. Likewise, “**Stepwise validity is narrower than algorithmic correctness**” (`:61`) correctly separates local checks from termination, completeness, optimality, and goal success. The prose also correctly keeps BFWS as trace/corpus evidence (`:69,78`), and states A*, visual/multimodal conditions, DAgger, and final evaluation as prospective (`:56,71,81`).

## Findings

- **CRITICAL — the only model result supplies no evidence for the title's “learn” claim or for a publishable learned-search contribution.** The abstract reports that “**a random-valid reference also achieved 1.0**” and that the learned policy showed “**no gain over the strongest control**” (`manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex:56`). The Introduction repeats that “**the absolute gain and paired-bootstrap lower bound over the best control were both zero**” (`:67`). This is admirably candid, but fatal to the current paper's contribution story: the headline asks whether VLMs *learn* to execute classical search, while the sole trained comparison says the learned system does not outperform a random-valid policy on the entire selected panel. “**executable BFS behavior**” (`:67,77`) then reduces to matching an unspecified random policy on 15 development tasks, not evidence that learning caused useful search control. A negative result could be publishable only if it identified a surprising mechanism or a discriminative hard regime; no such result or analysis is written. The current material is an honest stopped pilot, not an ICLR-level empirical answer.

- **MAJOR — “random-valid,” the control that invalidates the claimed learned advantage, is never defined, making the central comparison uninterpretable.** The text says “**random-valid also solved every selected task**” (`:67`) and calls it “**the strongest control**” (`:56`), but provides no definition of its sampling distribution, what makes a choice “valid,” whether it receives the same candidate facts and budget as process SFT, or whether the runtime filters/retries its draws. Those details determine whether 1.0 is a meaningful control, a privileged oracle, or a trivially solvable BFS setup. Since the zero-gain `VALID_STOP` rests entirely on this comparator, the reader cannot evaluate the most important scientific conclusion from the prose actually provided. Naming a control is not a substitute for specifying the intervention it controls for.

- **MAJOR — literal citation placeholders leave the motivation and novelty claim academically unusable.** “**Classical planning systems place search control in a deterministic algorithm \emph{[TODO: cite]}**,” followed by three more “**[TODO: cite]**” claims (`:63`), is not an acceptable related-work framing in an ICLR submission. The cautious sentence “**they do not justify a universal novelty claim**” (`:63`) avoids the prior strawman, but it does not establish the narrower novelty either. No reader can tell whether replay-determinative action interfaces already exist in neural algorithm execution, tool-use agents, or planning benchmarks. This is especially damaging because the paper presently offers an interface, a failed-to-separate-from-random pilot, and a corpus as its entire story; without citations, there is no evidence that the interface is novel or needed.

- **MAJOR — the abstract overstates what a 15-task development-panel result can demonstrate by calling it a VLM result without identifying the model or the test relationship.** “**final process-SFT checkpoints from five training seeds achieved 1.0 invariant-valid success**” (`:56`) and “**The first governed model result now comes from BFS**” (`:67`) give neither the model identity/scale nor whether the 15 tasks are disjoint from the process-SFT training material. “**outcome-blind**” (`:56,67`) only says outcomes were hidden when the panel was frozen; it does not establish training-test separation. The paper does disclose that this is a development panel and bounds its difficulty composition (`:67`), which is good, but 1.0 on 15 tasks—11 explicitly “**easy**” (`:67`)—cannot support the broad VLM framing without these basics. Reported precision (1.0, 0.0, and a bootstrap statement) makes the omission more conspicuous, not less.

- **MAJOR — the future empirical plan knowingly eliminates training-run replication yet still promises comparative model conditions.** “**each future model/cell configuration receives one training run at seed 17**” (`:71`) while “**repeated rollout or reference seeds ... do not estimate training-seed variance**” (`:71`). This is refreshingly explicit, but it means any future process-SFT-versus-DAgger, BFWS, A*, or modality difference is confounded with one training realization. The prose cannot present one seed as a comparative learning effect, even with many rollout seeds or bootstrap intervals. Since the remaining proposed stages are the only route to a positive scientific contribution, this budget decision severely limits the credibility of the promised paper.

- **MINOR — the contribution list inflates corpus qualification into a “structural-search substrate” without explaining why it changes the scientific question.** “**A BFWS structural-search substrate**” provides “**replay-verified exact BFWS traces**” and a “**leakage-audited text corpus**” (`:78`), while the preceding paragraph concedes “**BFWS training and all later algorithm and modality conclusions remain pending**” (`:78`). The evidence boundary is accurately stated, but a trace release is not by itself an ML contribution of sufficient excitement. The reader is left with no answer to why 69,019 exact decisions are substantively different from a well-engineered imitation corpus, particularly after the BFS imitation-style result failed to beat random-valid.

- **MINOR — terminology is mostly repaired, but “invariant-valid success” still bundles measurements the paper insists are distinct.** “**1.0 invariant-valid success with zero invalid operations**” (`:56`) and “**1.0 invariant-valid success and zero invalid operations**” (`:77`) do not tell the reader whether success is first computed and then conditioned on an all-invariant trace, whether invariant compliance is episode-level, or whether “valid” adds anything beyond the separately reported zero-invalid rate. This conflicts with the contribution's promise that “**Operation validity, invariant compliance, [and] episode success ... remain separate measurements**” (`:76`). State the three quantities separately; otherwise the headline result uses a composite label precisely where interpretability matters.

## Missing (not reviewed)

Method, experimental design, results, related work, limitations, and appendix content are unwritten; therefore the control definition, model specification, split contract, and statistical protocol invoked above cannot yet be independently assessed.

## Verdict

**REJECT.** The single most damaging remaining issue is that the only governed learned-policy result has zero gain over random-valid on the 15-task development panel, so the manuscript provides no evidence for its headline claim that a VLM *learns* useful classical-search execution (`manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex:56,67`).
