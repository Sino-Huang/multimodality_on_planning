## Critical Findings

- **CRITICAL — stale evidence boundary / logical contradiction.** `writing_design_tree.md:7` says, “**No current Search Episode Harness, Trusted Search Runtime, Typed Search Operation API, Search Memory contract, A* variants, Search Process Policy training data, trained policy, or model-efficacy result exists. All substantive tickets #41--#108 remain open.**” This is flatly incompatible with `content_brief.md:17`, which says fetched `origin/main` at `e3830e0` “contains a bounded BFS-only, text-state pilot with a Search Episode Harness, Typed Search Operations and Search Memory, a qualified 90-task train/dev corpus, and process-SFT machinery.” The alignment record explicitly identifies the former absence claim as stale (`main-implementation-manuscript-alignment.md:23`) and Oracle Gate 1 required reconciliation (`:27`). Leaving this sentence in the supposedly settled design tree makes the manuscript's feasibility story self-contradictory: either these components exist as a bounded pilot or they do not. The qualification must be preserved—BFS-only/text-state, train/dev-only, no scientific completion, machinery rather than trained policy—but the categorical absence cannot survive.

## Non-critical Findings

- **MAJOR — the document is a proposal architecture, not research-paper-ready evidence.** The blueprint promises a “**four-arm staged evaluation**” (`writing_design_tree.md:52,56`) and a primary “**algorithm by training arm**” results table (`:57`), while the same document admits that arm-level data, trace construction, compute parity, checkpoint selection, seeds, budget, splits, estimands, uncertainty, and thresholds are undefined (`:40-47`). This is honest, but it means the proposed comparison has no fixed estimand and cannot support a Results section or any causal contrast between operational SFT, process SFT, and DAgger. Calling it “**a result-ready empirical skeleton**” (`:14`; `content_brief.md:34`) is generous to the point of misleading; it is a section outline awaiting a protocol.

- **MAJOR — the headline materially outruns the only implementation evidence.** “**Can Vision-Language Models Learn to Execute Classical Search Algorithms?**” (`content_brief.md:22`; `writing_design_tree.md:51`) advertises VLMs and plural classical algorithms. The evidence summary immediately limits the only new pilot to “**BFS-only, text-state**” (`content_brief.md:17`), with IW/A*, modality matrix, and final evaluation explicitly unrun (`:28`). As a question it is not an unauthorized result claim, but it invites a broad multimodal/multi-algorithm contribution that the present program has not even operationalized under a frozen protocol. Without results, the so-what is merely a familiar proposal—models emit checked actions under an executor—not evidence that this solves the advertised VLM-search question.

- **MINOR — contribution wording blurs proposal and demonstrated contribution.** The Introduction is instructed to state “**three contributions: the Search Process Policy formulation, the no-repair runtime and planned evaluation protocol, and evidence reported only after gates run**” (`writing_design_tree.md:53`). The last item is not a contribution available now; it is a condition for a future contribution. This creates a tired-reader speed bump and risks turning the introduction into a promise ledger. The text should maintain the glossary's distinction between “Planned method component” and a demonstrated finding (`manuscript/CONTEXT.md:55-59`).

## Verdict

**WEAK REJECT.** The claim discipline in the content brief is largely sound and explicitly denies efficacy, but the design tree retains a directly stale, categorical denial of the BFS pilot components. The single most damaging issue is the contradiction between `writing_design_tree.md:7` and the fetched-main-aligned evidence in `content_brief.md:17`.
