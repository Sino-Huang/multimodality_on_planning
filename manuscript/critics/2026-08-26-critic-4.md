## Findings

- **MAJOR — #54 governance is inaccurately attributed and an absolute absence claim erases the retained history.** Both documents say, “**Issue #54 records corpus-release qualification `PASS` with `scientific_completion=false`**” (`manuscript/writing_design_tree.md:9`; `manuscript/content_brief.md:20`). At `12031ef`, however, the v6 freeze identifies the qualification as source issue **#111** and authorizes #54 as a downstream issue; the #54 comment says the active attempt is not retainable because its manifest has 21 rather than 16 accepted deltas. The same sentences then say, “**No clean governed SFT outcome exists**.” That is false without an explicit **v6/current-attempt** qualifier: #54 retains a historical v4 clean `VALID_STOP`. The documents correctly say #54 is open/reopened and correctly describe the 21-versus-16 defect and byte-identical learning payload, but they collapse three distinct facts—#111 corpus qualification, historical #54 adjudication, and the unretainable current v6 attempt—into one misleading status claim. This is exactly the kind of governance slippage that lets a reader mistake corpus `PASS` for a #54 experimental result.

- **MAJOR — the alleged IW/A* corpus “requirements” are technically underdetermined, despite being honestly labelled design inference.** “**A future IW corpus should expose the novelty and pruning information required by its Algorithm Invariant**” and “**A future A* corpus should expose the frontier-order quantities required by its declared heuristic, for example `g`, `h`, and `f`**” (`manuscript/writing_design_tree.md:56`; identically `manuscript/content_brief.md:32`) is not an executable corpus contract. IW legality depends on the declared feature vocabulary/width, novelty-table scope and update timing, duplicate policy, and successor ordering; A* legality additionally depends on an exact priority comparator (including tie-breaking), reopen/best-depth policy, heuristic values at the relevant state(s), and update timing. Merely serializing `g,h,f` leaves multiple legal-in-looking but behaviorally distinct frontier transitions. The documents do properly label this “**design inference, not implemented evidence**,” so this is not evidence inflation; it is nevertheless too vague to support their repeated promise of runtime-checked algorithm execution (`writing_design_tree.md:24,54`).

- **MINOR — “no trained” is stronger than the evidence boundary warrants.** “**No trained Search Process Policy ... exists**” (`manuscript/writing_design_tree.md:10`) and “**does not establish a trained Search Process Policy [or] checkpoint**” (`manuscript/content_brief.md:22`) are presented adjacent to the comment’s statement that “**the running model is consuming**” the v6 examples. The defensible claim is no *retainable governed v6 outcome* and no efficacy finding—not categorical nonexistence of a trained/running artifact. The second wording is less bad because it says “does not establish,” but both should distinguish artifact existence from valid retained scientific evidence.
