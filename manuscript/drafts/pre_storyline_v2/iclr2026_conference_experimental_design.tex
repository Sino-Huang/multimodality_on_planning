% Paragraph purpose: Say that two oracle-assisted controls bound every learned result, and define random-valid under each contract.
Every learned result is read against two oracle-assisted controls: the exact reference, which runs the declared algorithm, and random-valid. Both read the runtime's valid-operation list or the reference trace, so their scores are bounds, not abilities, and a saturated control leaves any gain unmeasurable rather than zero. Under the enumeration contract, random-valid is uniform over valid operations, drawing one listed candidate per decision, seeded, with no retry or repair. The candidates are successor rows under additive search, non-duplicate evaluated successors at an admissible frontier position under BFWS, and unvisited successors under BFS, else a frontier-retire operation. Under the choice-frontier contract, it is uniform over the offered frontier.

% Paragraph purpose: Say why the identity audit comes first and what role it plays under each contract.
The identity audit compares random-valid with the exact reference decision by decision, because where they never diverge, success measures validity, not choice. It is a pre-registered pass condition on the choice-frontier contract, passed by any divergence over frozen task-algorithm pairs without re-runs, and diagnostic on the enumeration contract's additive cells.

% Paragraph purpose: Say what the random-valid screen selects and how the unscreened panel removes that selection.
The validation and held-out panels pass a random-valid screen that admits a task only if 1 to 9 of its 10 episodes reach the goal, keeping tasks on which near-uniform choice sometimes succeeds. The unscreened panel (P2u), frozen with the held-out panel before evaluation, removes this selection by drawing kept candidates outside the held-out panel without the screen.

% Paragraph purpose: Give the validate-before-score procedure as four imperative steps, each with its rule and threshold (Box 1).
\begin{protocolbox}[!t]
\caption{Validate before score. Four steps admit random-valid as a choice control and validate the success measure before any policy is scored.}
\label{box:procedure}
\begin{tcolorbox}[
  title={Four-step validation},
  colbacktitle=black!60,
  coltitle=white,
  colback=black!3,
  colframe=black!60,
  fonttitle=\bfseries,
  fontupper=\small,
  boxrule=0.5pt,
  boxsep=5pt]
\textbf{Inputs.} The executed interface (per-decision candidate set, ordering, tie-break rule), the exact reference, and seeded random-valid with no retry or repair.

\smallskip
\textbf{1. Identity audit.} Compare random-valid with the exact reference decision by decision on the same tasks and seeds, reporting the divergent fraction and first divergence. Read success as choice only where decisions diverge and random-valid's success falls below the reference's. Otherwise success measures validity, which saturation alone cannot reveal (Section~\ref{sec:results-identity}).

\smallskip
\textbf{2. Budget check.} If a zero-token control exists, follow the compute-matched-baseline principle \citep{li2019random}. Count the budget in the units that the control spends, decisions or expansions, and report its success at the reference's count and at twice it. The check fails if the control saturates in its native unit but not in decisions (Section~\ref{sec:results-external}).

\smallskip
\textbf{3. Ladder.} If the reference computes a priority order, freeze a fresh panel before evaluation and pre-register the order exact reference, $\epsilon$ 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, random-valid. Each exact-$\epsilon$ rung replaces a fraction $\epsilon$ of reference decisions with random-valid choices. Score M1, the area under the solve-versus-budget curve up to twice the reference's expansion count. The ladder passes only if all four adjacent paired task-clustered 95\% lower bounds are positive along the $\epsilon$ axis. The rungs are privileged, so a pass validates the measurement, not a policy (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}).

\smallskip
\textbf{4. Report.} Give the policy's difference from random-valid with a paired task-clustered interval and a pre-registered margin (0.05 here), its ladder position, per-task signs, screened and unscreened panels, and training seeds. Non-separation from a rung is not equivalence (Section~\ref{sec:results-adapter}).

\smallskip
\textbf{Where a step was at risk (one audited instance each).} Complete-set submission with a priority heap and sorted tie-breaking failed step~1 (our enumeration contract's additive cells, 48/48). Per-step choice among valid actions passed step~1 (ScienceWorld). Model-scored candidates on a frontier that keeps every alternative, under a token budget, failed step~2 by construction (LLM-First Search).

\smallskip
\textbf{If a step fails,} change the contract (for example, let the policy choose the frontier state, or break ties in submission order) and audit again.
\end{tcolorbox}

% Paragraph purpose: Say that every choice-frontier verdict follows a rule frozen before its outcome, and name the rules.
Each choice-frontier verdict follows a rule frozen before its outcome. The ladder uses Box~\ref{box:procedure}'s pass rule. The adapter rule requires a positive difference from random-valid in M1, with a materiality margin of 0.05 and an equivalence margin of $\pm 0.05$. Later frozen protocols add three training seeds, two fresh panels, a zero-shot policy, and a second external audit.

% Paragraph purpose: Say what the enumeration-contract study compares, on which grid, and under which budget; defer recipes to the appendix.
The enumeration-contract study compares process SFT with the pretrained base, SFT plus two DAgger iterations \citep{ross2011dagger}, and an exposure-matched continued-SFT control. It trains low-rank adaptation (LoRA) adapters \citep{hu2022lora} on replay-verified examples with Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct \citep{bai2025qwen3vl} and uses InternVL3.5-8B \citep{wang2025internvl35} as a second backbone (Appendix~\ref{app:data-splits}).

The arms run on the expanded baseline of text, visual, and multimodal observations over 24 tasks from 12 domains, with one algorithm under one observation per cell. Budgets count decision calls, charge invalid operations, cap adapters at twice the exact reference's count, and price the base at one call. An episode ends at its first invalid emission and counts only after independent replay. Contrasts use a paired whole-instance bootstrap (Appendix~\ref{app:statistics}).
