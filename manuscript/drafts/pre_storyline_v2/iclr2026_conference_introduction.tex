\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph purpose: Pose the title question as a measurement problem and set up the policy, runtime, algorithms, contracts, and the natural control.
Can a vision-language model learn to execute a classical search algorithm?
Answering requires a success measure that responds to the model's choices (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}).
Prior probes evaluate language models on classical planning \citep{valmeekam2023critical,stein2025autoplanbench,kokel2026acpbenchhard}.
We instead train a Search Process Policy to emit Typed Search Operations, which a no-repair Trusted Search Runtime validates (Section~\ref{sec:policy}).
The declared algorithms are breadth-first search (BFS), Best-First Width Search (BFWS), and additive best-first search, guided by the additive heuristic $h_{\rm add}$, in greedy and weighted-A* variants.
Observations are text, visual, or multimodal under an enumeration contract and scene-only under a choice-frontier contract.
The natural control is random-valid, which chooses uniformly among valid operations.


% Paragraph purpose: Show why success can mislead: under the enumeration contract random-valid makes the reference's decisions, so every process-SFT success measures validity, not choice.
Success reflects choice only when random-valid decides differently from the exact reference (the declared algorithm run as specified). Under the enumeration contract, it need not.
Each expansion there submits its complete candidate set, and in the additive best-first cells random-valid makes exactly the reference's decisions on 48/48 task-algorithm pairs (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}).

On the expanded baseline, our main enumeration-contract grid (Section~\ref{sec:design}), every success of process supervised fine-tuning (SFT) falls in these cells, and SFT solves no BFS or BFWS task (Table~\ref{tab:results-identity}).

% Paragraph purpose: State the approach, validate the measurement before scoring: admit the choice-frontier contract by the identity audit and a decision-counted budget, then require M1 to pass the privileged ladder.
We therefore validate the measurement before scoring any policy, starting from the choice-frontier contract, where the policy picks which frontier state to expand.
This contract passes the identity audit, with random-valid diverging on 18/18 pairs (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}), and its budget counts decisions, following the compute-matched-baseline principle \citep{li2019random}.
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v1/evaluation/identity-audit.json pairs_divergent 18 of pairs_checked 18 (#132).
M1, the area under the solve-versus-budget curve, must then pass a pre-registered ladder of exact-$\epsilon$ selectors.
These replace a fraction $\epsilon$ of reference decisions with random-valid choices, so a valid M1 must order them by $\epsilon$.
M1 passes on the validation and held-out panels (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}).
% (moved out of prose; kept in Section 4, sec:design, validation-panel sentence) the validation panel's 12 tasks from 7 domains.
% Evidence: configs/experiments/choice-frontier-v2/membership.json task_ids (12), domains (7); outputs/choice-frontier/v2/metrics/analysis.json instrument_validity.verdict PASS (#135 E135-01..16).
% Evidence: #139 outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json pooled.panels.p2 11; ladder_p2.verdict PASS (confirmatory, issue-139-protocol.md L93).
Because the rungs read the reference's computed priority order, which no policy sees, the ladder validates the measurement, not a policy (Box~\ref{box:procedure}).

% Paragraph purpose: State the three contributions without numbers, the validate-before-score measurement first; each is a bold lead phrase and one plain sentence.
We make three contributions, of which the first is primary.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,parsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{A validate-before-score measurement of search choice.} The identity audit and a decision-counted budget \citep{li2019random} admit the interface, and M1 passes a pre-registered privileged ladder before any policy is scored.
    \item \textbf{Evidence that admission is needed.} On one runtime, a tie-break change alone flips the audit's verdict, and every process-SFT success on the expanded baseline lies where success measures validity.
    \item \textbf{A worked use and a portable procedure (Box~\ref{box:procedure}).} The measurement scores the trained adapter above random-valid on three panels, and the procedure's first two steps run on two published interfaces, crediting the budget step to \citet{li2019random}.
\end{itemize}
