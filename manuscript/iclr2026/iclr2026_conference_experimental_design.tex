\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

% Paragraph purpose: Define the two oracle-assisted controls as bounds and pin the random-valid sampler under each contract.
Every learned result is read against two oracle-assisted controls, which read the runtime's valid-operation list or the reference trace, and never as an ability of those controls. The exact reference runs the declared algorithm and defines the imitation target. Where a control saturates, the gain is unmeasurable rather than zero. Under the enumeration contract, random-valid is uniform over valid operations, drawing one listed candidate per decision with a seeded generator and no retry or repair. Candidates are successor rows under additive search, non-duplicate evaluated successors at an admissible frontier position under BFWS, and unvisited successors under BFS, else a frontier-retire operation. Under the choice-frontier contract, random-valid is uniform over the offered frontier, so each random-valid number is read under its own contract.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the identity audit and its pre-registered role.
The identity audit compares random-valid with the exact reference decision by decision. It is the choice-frontier contract's pre-registered pass condition over 18 seed-17 pairs, passing on any divergence without re-runs, and a diagnostic on the enumeration contract's additive cells.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the held-out and unscreened panels once, with the held-out meaning and the unexecuted final evaluation.
The held-out panel (P2 in figures and tables) and the unscreened panel (P2u) were frozen from new seeds before any evaluation episode. Held-out means this panel only, built with the validation panel's rules from seeds on which no adapter had been evaluated. Its seed extension followed a controls-only screen of the first 40 seeds. The unscreened panel skips the screen and draws kept candidates outside the held-out panel. The frozen 45-task final evaluation remains unexecuted.

% Paragraph purpose: State the pre-registered ladder and adapter rules and the later frozen protocols.
Each choice-frontier result has a rule frozen before its outcome. The ladder test passes only if all four adjacent paired 95\% lower bounds are positive along the exact reference, the exact-$\epsilon$ rungs, and random-valid. Its intervals are task-clustered, resampling whole tasks (seed 133, 10,000 draws). The adapter rule requires adapter minus random-valid M1 to be positive with a materiality margin of 0.05 and sets an equivalence margin of $\pm 0.05$. Later protocols, each frozen before its outcomes, cover three seeds, two fresh panels, a zero-shot policy, and a second external audit.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the enumeration-contract conditions, recipe, backbones, and compute cap.
The enumeration-contract study compares process SFT with its controls. Each cell holds the pretrained base, process SFT, SFT plus two DAgger iterations \citep{ross2011dagger}, and an exposure-matched continued-SFT control. SFT trains a fresh low-rank adaptation (LoRA) adapter \citep{hu2022lora} for one epoch on 512 replay-verified records. The BFWS gate checkpoint used two epochs on 47,780 records. The backbone is Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct \citep{bai2025qwen3vl} (revision \texttt{0c351dd0}), with InternVL3.5-8B \citep{wang2025internvl35} second. All runs share a 336 GPU-hour cap.

% Paragraph purpose: Fix splits, the matrix panel, budget, termination, and the analysis contract.
The modality matrix is the enumeration-contract evaluation grid of text, visual, and multimodal observations over 24 tasks from 12 domains. Across the three observations this gives 288 episodes per arm. Budgets count decision calls, charge invalid operations, cap adapters at twice the exact reference's count, and price the base at one call. An episode ends at its first invalid emission and counts only after independent replay. Contrasts use a paired whole-instance bootstrap (10,000 resamples, seed 1729, Statistics appendix). Transfer uses exact McNemar tests with Holm correction across 36 comparisons.
