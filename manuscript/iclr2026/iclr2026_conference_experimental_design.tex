\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

% Paragraph purpose: State the comparative question, the staged gate logic, and the development-stage boundary that governs every executed stage.
The study is organized around one comparative question per cell, asked of every algorithm-by-modality-by-condition combination: whether a trained Search Process Policy exceeds an oracle-assisted validity control on full-episode budgeted success under the ownership contract of Section~\ref{sec:policy}. Training and evaluation run under frozen protocols and a 336 GPU-hour program cap, so the work proceeds as a staged sequence in which each stage is admitted by a cost gate and closed by a prespecified outcome (PASS, VALID\_STOP, INVALID, or ANCESTOR\_STOP) that is preserved as a receipt. Every executed stage is development-stage evidence. Panels are frozen and outcome-blind, the held-out manifest has never been accessed, and all model comparisons use a single training seed per cell except the two cells replicated under issue \#127. We report the design with its gate statuses intact, including the stages that stopped early, because the stop receipts bound what the evidence supports.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the active algorithm matrix and record the retired optimal A* pair with its reason.
Four declared algorithms form the active matrix. Breadth-first search (BFS) provides the minimal frontier discipline. The structural gate uses the complete unpruned variant of best-first width search \citep{lipovetzky2017bestfirst}, which orders the frontier by novelty and goal count without pruning. Two additive best-first variants complete the matrix, weighted A* with weight 3 \citep{pohl1970heuristic} and greedy best-first, both over the additive heuristic $h_{\rm add}$ \citep{bonet2001planning} and named \texttt{astar\_w3} and \texttt{astar\_greedy} in the transfer branch. An earlier stage paired two optimal A* variants that differed in heuristic representation, one over $h_{\max}$ and one over a landmark-count heuristic. That pair is retired. A governed VALID\_STOP under issue \#62 ended the stage before corpus construction, its heuristic-representation estimand remains unidentified, and no optimal A* arm participates in the active design.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the three checkpoint conditions and distinguish gate-era from expanded-program training recipes, including backbones.
Each algorithm-by-modality cell holds three checkpoint conditions. The base condition is the pretrained model with no task-specific optimization. In the expanded program, the process-SFT condition trains a fresh LoRA adapter \citep{hu2022lora} on replay-verified Search-Trace Segments, using 512 unique records for one epoch (16 optimizer updates) per cell. Targets are the exact teacher's Typed Search Operations. The BFWS structural-gate checkpoint predates this recipe. It trained on the full 47,780-record training corpus for two epochs (seed 17). The additive development panels (issues \#65 and \#66) likewise used two-epoch full-corpus runs. The third condition continues a process-SFT checkpoint with two iterations of DAgger \citep{ross2011dagger}, in which the exact teacher relabels the states that the learned policy actually visits. An exposure-matched continued-SFT arm receives the same added training exposure and controls for it. The primary backbone is Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct \citep{bai2025qwen3vl} at pinned revision \texttt{0c351dd0}, and the second-backbone stage trains InternVL3.5-8B \citep{wang2025internvl35} under the same expanded-program cell recipe.

% Paragraph purpose: State the seed policy and separate the historical five-seed pilot from it.
Exactly one training seed is authorized per model/cell configuration under the supervisor budget, and every executed cell after the pilot uses seed 17, except the two cells selected for prospective training-seed replication (issue \#127, protocol frozen 2026-09-20) that added seeds 29 and 71. The historical BFS pilot predates this policy. It trained five process-SFT seeds on the bounded v8 corpus and evaluated all five on its development panel. That five-seed run is retained as pilot history and is not a template for later cells. Outside the pilot and the two replication cells, no cell estimates training-seed variance, and repeated rollout or reference seeds do not substitute for a training seed.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the random-valid and exact-classical references as oracle-assisted bounds, with the missing random-valid specification marked.
Two programmatic references bracket every learned arm. The exact-classical reference is the declared algorithm itself, executed by the Trusted Search Runtime with replay verification under the same budget, and it bounds perfect operation-level decision-making. The random-valid reference is an oracle-assisted control that proposes only operations the runtime will accept as valid, so it bounds what valid-operation bookkeeping alone achieves without learned state evaluation. Neither reference is a learned ability. When a reference saturates a panel, the learned-to-control gain on that panel is unmeasurable rather than zero. The frozen protocol has not yet pinned the validity-constrained sampling distribution, the candidate access granted to the control, the runtime filtering step, or the explicit absence of retry and repair. These details are owed to the evidence contract before the final evaluation can freeze.

% Paragraph purpose: Specify whole-instance splits and give the frozen panel inventory with exact counts.
All splits are by whole task instance, so no state, decision, or target from an evaluation task appears in any training row, and each released corpus passed no-overlap, no-conflict, live-parity, and token-budget audits before use. The panel inventory is frozen. The BFS pilot used an outcome-blind 15-task development panel. Its released text-state process corpus holds 25,109 process rows (12,994 train, 12,115 dev) drawn from 90 replay-verified traces. The BFWS gate froze a 105-task development panel and a 45-task held-out manifest that remains unaccessed, and its 69,019 replay-verified teacher decisions project to 47,780 train and 21,239 dev process rows. The modality matrix evaluates 24 tasks from 12 domains under three Modality Observations (text-state, visual-state, multimodal-state), which gives 288 episodes per arm across the four algorithms and 1,152 bindings across the four arms. The generalization stage admits 25 frozen semantics-preserving perturbation variants, yielding 150 learned-adapter episodes inside 1,200 replayed episodes. The observation-corruption suite freezes $k=9$ source tasks. The transfer stage runs 36 paired comparisons.

% Paragraph purpose: Fix the budget unit, budget parity, the termination rule, and the separated measures.
Episodes are budgeted in decision calls, and invalid operations are charged to the budget. Budget parity holds inside each cell. Learned adapters are capped at twice the exact-reference decision count of the task, the base condition is priced at one call per episode, and the programmatic references run on CPU under the same frozen rollout rules. Under those rules an episode terminates on its first invalid emission, so model arms have no retry or repair path. A model episode counts only after an independent replay of its raw emissions reproduces the recorded trace. Full-episode budgeted success is the primary measure. Operation validity, Algorithm Invariant compliance, invalid-operation rate, and charged budget are reported separately, and no stage combines them into one score.

% Paragraph purpose: Fix the analysis contract: paired bootstrap, corrected transfer tests, and the tiny-strata rule.
Contrasts are paired by whole task instance. Unless a stage's frozen protocol states otherwise, paired differences use a bootstrap over instances with 10,000 resamples at seed 1729 and 95\% percentile intervals, transfer comparisons use exact McNemar tests against the base with Holm correction across the 36 comparisons, and strata with fewer than 8 instances are reported as descriptive only. Under one training seed per cell (three in the two replicated cells) and panels of this size, these intervals are descriptive bounds and cannot support broad superiority claims.

Each was frozen before its first outcome. The paragraphs below define the protocol elements that Section~\ref{sec:results} reports for them.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the native-arms v2 observation contract.
The native-arms protocol (issues \#128/\#129) adds two image-only observation arms to the additive cells. Both reduce the textual payload to the static task interface plus the grounded, unscored candidate menu in trusted grounded order. Textual Search Memory, accepted deltas, the current $g$, $h$, and priority scalars, and every candidate score and membership column are removed, and a structural gate scans the serialized text for each removed key before use. In \texttt{visual-nomem-state} (nomem), state facts arrive only as the current rendered scene. \texttt{visual-seq-state} (seq) adds a history window of $K{=}8$ rendered predecessor states from the current state's first-acceptance provenance path, in visit order. Each arm and algorithm trains one adapter under the frozen expanded-program recipe at seed 17.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the superseded v1 gate and the pre-registered v2 smoke gate, plus the corruption leak rule.
The \#128 v1 admission gate required the pretrained base to emit schema-valid grounded first actions at a rate of at least 0.5 on an 18-call probe. It measured base format compliance, stopped both arms with \textsc{valid\_stop}, and is retained as gate history. The v2 amendment, frozen before any trained adapter existed, replaced it with a pre-registered post-training smoke gate. Each trained arm runs the three cheapest tasks of the frozen 9-task membership under both algorithms, and the arm proceeds only if its schema-valid grounded call rate (runtime-accepted calls over model calls) is at least 0.5. The clean panel crosses the 9 tasks with both algorithms. The corruption cells apply the \#126 visual-blank and visual-degraded families to every attached image of the seq arm, including history frames. Because images are then the only state and history channel, the protocol declared in advance that a non-material corruption effect would be reported as a leak signal.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the menu-manipulation protocol and its endpoint.
Menu manipulation (issue \#130, R1) tests what a trained policy reads from the grounded menu. It evaluates the frozen \#129 adapters of both native arms, with no retraining, on the 9-task panel under both additive algorithms. Menu permutation reorders the menu entries with a seeded Fisher--Yates shuffle and keeps every entry intact and copyable. Distractor injection inserts up to $k{=}3$ schema-valid but currently inapplicable grounded actions per decision at seeded positions, drawn from the task's scene-catalog actions minus the applicable set. The runtime still validates against the true state, so emitting a distractor is an invalid operation and ends the episode. The endpoint is the distractor pick-rate over emitted operations at decisions that carry injected distractors, and the episode's first emitted operation is recorded with it.

% Paragraph purpose: Set the two decision contracts side by side.
Table~\ref{tab:design-contracts} sets the enumeration contract, under which every main-program stage runs, beside the choice-frontier contract of issue \#132. The redesign inverts ownership of the frontier. The policy no longer enumerates candidate actions into a frontier kept in textual Search Memory. It selects which runtime-owned frontier state to expand next, and the runtime performs that expansion atomically with the frozen additive admission, duplicate, and reopen rules. The binding budget caps decisions and expansions at 2$\times$ the additive exact-reference expansion count, and selecting a goal state ends the episode without charge, so exact reference solves by construction and serves as the imitation target.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the per-decision identity audit as a named check and its pre-registered verdict rule.
The identity audit is the check both contracts share. For each (task, algorithm) control pair it compares the random-valid episode against the exact-reference episode, and a pair is divergent when the expanded-state sequences or the decision and expansion counts differ. On the enumeration contract it runs over the additive cells of the expanded baseline. On the choice-frontier contract it is the redesign's own pre-registered pass condition. The seed-17 random-valid episode of each of the 18 control pairs is compared with exact reference, one or more divergent pairs return \texttt{CHOICE\_SENSITIVE}, and zero divergent pairs return \texttt{ZERO\_DECISION\_HEADROOM} as the reported outcome with no re-runs or variant search.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the choice-frontier panel, conditions, and gates.
The choice-frontier evaluation reuses the frozen 9-task native-arms membership under both additive algorithms. Each (task, algorithm) cell runs the learned adapter and exact reference at seed 17, the pretrained base under a hard one-call cap, and random-valid under five frozen rollout seeds, for 144 episodes. Two adapters, one per algorithm, train on a corpus re-derived through the new contract on CPU from the stored exact traces, with the teacher target set to the label of the heap-head state. The smoke gate repeats the native-arms rule on the three cheapest panel tasks at threshold 0.5. Contrasts use the paired conventions of Appendix~\ref{appendix}.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the #133 comparator-zoo arms, cap, and oracle-access asymmetry.
The comparator zoo (issue \#133) is a CPU-only exploratory protocol, frozen before any comparator episode existed, that calibrates the choice-frontier measurement against named selectors. Its unit is the nine panel tasks, and the two additive cells are repeated measures inside each task cluster. Every primary episode runs at the frozen shared cap of 2$\times$ the additive exact-reference expansion count, never rescaled to a comparator's own reference cost. Three deterministic selectors run at seed 17 through the learned adapter's menu, admission, and budget path: \texttt{bfs-order} (minimum frontier $g$, then earliest generation serial), novelty-first selection (lowest width-1 then width-2 novelty over positive PDDL state atoms, then earliest serial), and \texttt{worst-first} (maximum additive priority, then earliest serial) as a negative control. Novelty-first is a selection rule only and never BFWS, because it performs no novelty pruning. Random-valid is the floor and exact reference the imitation target. Random-valid and the three selectors also run at 3$\times$ and 4$\times$ caps as control runs outside the primary metric. The selectors read trusted $g$/$h$ scalars or PDDL atoms that the model never sees, so the comparison is not information-matched. They also act on the additive frontier and are not the native BFS or BFWS algorithms.
% Evidence: docs/experiments/choice-frontier/issue-133-protocol.md (arms, tie-breaks, frozen 2x cap, 3x/4x runs, oracle-access asymmetry).

% Paragraph purpose: Define the #133 primary metric, uncertainty, claim unit, and test family.
The primary metric M1 is the trapezoidal area under the solve fraction over budget multipliers 1, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, and 2, with random seeds averaged within cell. Its uncertainty comes from a task-cluster bootstrap that draws nine tasks with replacement at seed 133 for 10,000 draws. M2 (expansion overhead against the reference) and M3 (plan cost against an offline optimum) are reported on co-solved cells and are selection-conditioned. The claim unit is Pareto dominance across M1, M2, and M3. A positive claim requires weak superiority on all three paired measures and strict superiority on one, and it is tested against five comparators on three metrics with Holm correction across the resulting fifteen tests. The remaining metrics are diagnostics, all \#133 metrics on the stored \#132 episodes are post-hoc exploratory, and non-significance licenses no equivalence claim.
