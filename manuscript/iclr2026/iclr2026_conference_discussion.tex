\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

% Paragraph purpose: State what the measurement shows, the identity audit with both verdicts and then the validated choice-frontier metric with its privileged-rung boundary.
On the additive best-first cells of our enumeration contract, where an expansion submits its complete candidate set and heap serials follow the sorted candidate order, random-valid is decision-identical to the exact reference on 48/48 pairs, so success there measures operation validity rather than choice.
% Evidence: outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit.json pairs_identical 48; identity-audit-submission-order.json rules.sorted_candidate_order.pairs_identical 48 (#134 E134-03).
On the same runtime with heap serials assigned in submission order, the same check finds 19/48 divergent pairs, so the check can return either verdict. The pre-registered prediction of divergence on most pairs (more than 24/48) did not hold.
% Evidence: outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit-submission-order.json rules.submission_order.pairs_divergent 19; preregistered_prediction.held false (#134 E134-02, E134-04, E134-07).
The redesigned contract's M1 passes the pre-registered ladder test (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}). The rungs read the privileged heap head, so the ladder validates the ordering of the measurement, not any realisable policy.
Menu-manipulation and zoo results on the earlier contracts are in the appendix (Appendix~\ref{sec:results-menu}).

% Paragraph purpose: State the learned result and its boundaries, including the screened-panel and concentration caveat.
A scaled adapter beats uniform choice on this panel, $D = +0.328$ $[+0.155, +0.508]$, which meets the pre-registered POSITIVE rule for its single training seed. Its M1 of 0.349 sits at the exact-$\epsilon$ 0.75 rung (0.347) on point estimates, far below the 0.50 rung (0.603).
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v3/metrics/analysis.json primary.D, primary.ci95, primary.verdict POSITIVE, primary.seeds [17]; per_seed.17.m1_auc; ladder_position.learned.17.* (#136 E136-01..07, E136-20, E136-33).
This is a development-stage, single-seed result on one screened panel, obtained by imitating the exact reference, and it supports no planning-ability, generality, or held-out claim.
Screening admitted only tasks where random-valid is discriminative, and the effect is concentrated, with 4 of the 12 tasks at or below zero on the per-task difference (Section~\ref{sec:results-adapter}).

The contribution is therefore the measurement, not the exposure of a common flaw.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Paragraph purpose: Give the scoped answer, the validated measurement and learned result at its rung, and the claim frame.
On our runtime a per-decision identity check returns both verdicts. The redesigned choice-frontier measurement passes a pre-registered ladder test, and a scaled adapter beats uniform choice with M1 at the exact-$\epsilon$ 0.75 rung on point estimates. The choice-quality claim is development-stage and single-seed, holds on the validation panel relative to uniform choice and the exact-$\epsilon$ ladder, and carries no planning-ability, generality, or held-out claim.
