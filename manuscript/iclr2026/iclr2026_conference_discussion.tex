\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

% Paragraph purpose: State the measurement arc (finding, audit, fix, first measurement) and what it does and does not show, with the scoped finding-1/2 wording.
% Evidence: outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit.json (48/48, additive pairs only); outputs/native-arms/v1/stress-evaluation.json
% and the distractor episode records (pick-rate at chance, review 16 finding 2); outputs/choice-frontier/v1/evaluation/{identity-audit,analysis}.json.
The results form a measurement finding and an audit-driven fix. On the additive best-first cells of the enumeration contract, choice quality is unmeasurable by construction, because an expansion must submit its complete candidate set and heap order ignores submission order, while the BFS and width-based best-first cells register choice and the learned policy never succeeds there (Section~\ref{sec:results-identity}). Menu permutation is therefore uninformative about order, and distractor injection shows only that the policy does not reject schema-valid inapplicable menu entries (pick-rate at chance). The choice-frontier redesign separates random-valid from exact reference, and its first measurement shows contract fluency rather than choice quality. Its contrast against random-valid rests on one discriminative task and, without a pre-registered margin, licenses neither superiority nor equivalence. The evidence supports no learning-to-plan claim for the redesigned arm.

% Paragraph purpose: Interpret the enumeration-contract evidence and the runtime boundary that limits every positive result.
Every positive result depends on the Trusted Search Runtime, which never chooses operands or repairs them, so decisions are attributable to the model and the evidence does not establish unassisted internal search or general planning autonomy. Within that boundary the additive-cell output contract is learnable (BFS and BFWS cells score 0/24), a second backbone replicates this contract fluency, and the one positive learned-control contrast (the BFWS development panel) remains a single-seed development-stage finding. After process supervision, most learned emissions parse, and the dominant failure is an invariant rejection. The language-channel dependence is supported only by the copyability-preserving shuffled condition. As a post-hoc interpretation, the menu is the only retained input that could carry the operation-validity signal on this panel. The DAgger, transfer, and curriculum studies returned nulls, the evidence does not support an intrinsic modality effect, and the comparator zoo finds no positive learned Pareto dominance, which is not a general negative capability claim.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Development stage.} Held-out manifest unaccessed, single seed except two cells ($n{=}3$).
\item \emph{Saturation.} Saturating controls bound measurability, not policy quality.
\item \emph{Exposure.} Longer gate-era training confounds the missing BFWS positive.
\item \emph{Imitation target.} \texttt{exact\_reference} solves by construction of the budget.
\item \emph{Comparators.} Zoo selectors read privileged inputs, so ranking is not information-matched.
\item \emph{Menu and nulls.} No arm removes the menu, and ceiling nulls do not show image irrelevance.
\item \emph{Panel reuse.} Adaptive-overfitting risk on one reused panel requires a fresh frozen panel.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Paragraph purpose: Give the gate-bounded answer, the zoo boundary, and the single next gated step.
Grounded-menu search evaluations can leave choice quality unmeasurable by construction, and a cheap per-decision identity audit exposes this. The choice-frontier redesign separates the controls, and the adapter learns to emit valid choices without demonstrated choice quality. Its solve-versus-budget score lies below random-valid (paired $-0.035$ $[-0.150, +0.061]$, which is not equivalence), and a post-hoc label audit finds 54.5\% last-label emissions. These development-stage findings establish neither general efficacy, an intrinsic modality effect, nor planning autonomy. The next gated step is a held-out evaluation on the frozen 45-task manifest and a fresh choice-frontier panel (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
