\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Paragraph purpose: Restate the question at two levels, give the development-stage answer (execution learnable but data-hungry; node choice learnable from images; a transfer hint), and end on what it means for planning research.
We asked whether a vision-language model can learn classical search from its traces, both by executing an algorithm and by choosing which node to expand. It can do both: it learns to execute BFS and BFWS, and it learns to choose the next node for greedy best-first search and weighted A* from images alone, well above random choice on held-out tasks. Training on width-based traces also points beyond planning, raising logical-reasoning accuracy on FOLIO. Coupling the two levels toward a VLM that carries an entire search is the natural next step, and because the supervision comes from a planner's traces rather than plans, the community's existing domains, planners, and benchmarks can endow VLMs with domain-independent search behavior directly.
