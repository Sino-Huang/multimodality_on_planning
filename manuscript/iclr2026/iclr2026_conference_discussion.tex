\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

% Paragraph purpose: State the identity audit's two verdicts on one runtime, and what the ladder licenses.
The identity audit returns both verdicts on one runtime, so it must run on each interface before random-valid success is read as choice. On the enumeration contract's additive cells it finds 48/48 identical pairs under sorted ties and 19/48 divergent pairs under submission order (Section~\ref{sec:results-identity}). The ladder licenses reading a higher M1 as better choice on the validation panel and the held-out panel (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}). Because the rungs read the reference's computed priority order, which is never shown to a policy, the ladder validates the measurement, not a policy.
% Evidence (moved from Results 5.2, already in the body): outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit.json pairs_identical 48 of 48; #134 outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit-submission-order.json rules.submission_order.pairs_divergent 19 of 48.

% Paragraph purpose: Interpret the three-panel learned result and its panel-dependent ladder position.
The adapter chooses better than uniform choice on every panel, but how far depends on the panel (Section~\ref{sec:results-adapter}). It is separated above the 0.75 rung only on the held-out panel. Its M1 of 0.151 to 0.432 stays well below the reference's 0.875.
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json arms.p2u.learned_adapter_seed_mean.m1 0.151, arms.p2.learned_adapter_seed_mean.m1 0.432, arms.*.exact_reference.m1 0.875 (rounded half-up to 3 dp from the named keys).
On the unscreened panel the gain rests on 4 of 12 tasks (descriptive). The gain comes from imitating the exact reference.
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json concentration.per_panel.p2u.counts positive 4, zero 8; unscreened_p2u.ladder.verdict PASS (descriptive); P2u 0/10 random-valid screening goals on all 12 tasks: docs/experiments/choice-frontier/issue-139-closeout.md L56 (#139).

% Paragraph purpose: State what each external audit exercises, and that the contribution is the measurement with its two checks.
The external audits exercise each check on a published interface. The identity audit passes on both, and on LLM-First Search the budget check fails by construction. On ScienceWorld \citep{wang2022scienceworld}, random-valid registers choice (Section~\ref{sec:results-external}). None of the seven surveyed published interfaces uses an enumeration contract, so the 48/48 identity failure is shown only on our runtime. On LLM-First Search, random-valid success saturates under the paper's token caps, which a zero-token chooser never exhausts, yet counted in expansions the reference's choices matter. The contribution is therefore the measurement and its two checks, not the exposure of one flaw.
% Evidence: docs/experiments/external-audit/issue-137-survey.md §Candidate table (7); issue-137-closeout.md §Stage 1 survey (0 type A) (#137 E137-40, E137-42); outputs/external-audit/v2/lfs/identity-audit.json pairs_identical 0, paper_budget.verdict SATURATED, matched_decision_budget.verdict CHOICE_REGISTERED (#142).

% Paragraph purpose: State the portable rule of the two checks.
The portable rule is that an interface must pass two checks before random-valid success is read as choice. The identity audit must find divergent pairs. The budget check must show that the success budget binds decisions, the compute-matched-baseline principle \citep{li2019random}. A budget that a zero-token chooser never exhausts, on a frontier that keeps every alternative, turns random-valid choice into exhaustive randomized search. When either check fails, success reports validity or search coverage, not choice. Under a passing contract, report a policy's difference from random-valid together with its ladder position.

% Paragraph purpose: List the limitations, one line each.
\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Ladder position.} The adapter is separated above the 0.75 rung only on the held-out panel, not on the validation panel, the unscreened panel, or pooled.
\item \emph{Screening.} The held-out panel is screened like the validation panel, and on the unscreened panel the gain rests on 4 of 12 tasks.
\item \emph{Seeds.} There are three training seeds, and seed 29 is weakest on every panel (descriptive).
% Evidence: seed 29 lowest per panel: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/seeds/metrics/analysis.json primary.per_seed.29; outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json heldout_p2.primary.per_seed.29, unscreened_p2u.primary.per_seed.29 (#138, #139).
\item \emph{Final evaluation.} The frozen 45-task final evaluation is unexecuted.
\item \emph{Prediction.} The pre-registered majority prediction for submission-order divergence failed.
\item \emph{Runtime.} The runtime never chooses or repairs operands, so no result shows the model searching without the runtime's validation.
\item \emph{External audit.} The LLM-First Search reference uses a substituted local model, and the audit covers 80 tasks from four configurations.
% Evidence: outputs/external-audit/v2/lfs/identity-audit.json tasks 80, per_config {cd3, cd5, cd7, su4}; configs/experiments/external-audit-v2/lfs-reference-server.json .model (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct) (#142).
\item \emph{Second policy.} The second realisable policy is zero-shot, and the trained second backbone was not run under the choice-frontier contract (it ran at reduced scope under the enumeration contract).
\item \emph{Modality.} The choice-frontier results use one scene-only observation, so no modality is compared under that contract.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Paragraph purpose: Give the thesis as an answer, the two checks, the validated measurement, the learned result with its ladder position, and the claim frame.
We argue that random-valid success measures choice only on an interface that passes the identity audit and the budget check. Our runtime's identity audit returns both verdicts, and on LLM-First Search random-valid success saturates under the paper's token caps, which a zero-token chooser never exhausts. The measurement passes a pre-registered ladder test, and a trained adapter beats uniform choice across three seeds, $D_3 = +0.285$ $[+0.140, +0.433]$.
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/seeds/metrics/analysis.json primary.D3, primary.ci95 (#138).
On the held-out panel, $D_3 = +0.416$ $[+0.250, +0.591]$.
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json heldout_p2.primary.D3, heldout_p2.primary.ci95 (#139).
It is separated above the 0.75 rung on the held-out panel ($+0.227$ $[+0.053, +0.403]$) but not separated from that rung on the validation panel ($-0.041$ $[-0.187, +0.102]$), the unscreened panel, or pooled over 35 tasks ($+0.058$ $[-0.040, +0.156]$, descriptive).
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json heldout_p2.separation.vs_eps_0.75.S, .ci95; outputs/choice-frontier/v4/seeds/metrics/analysis.json separation.vs_eps_0.75.S, .ci95; v4/panels analysis.json unscreened_p2u.separation.vs_eps_0.75.verdict NOT_SEPARATED; pooled.tasks 35, pooled.separation_vs_eps_0.75.S, .ci95 (#138, #139).
The choice-quality claim is development-stage, holds relative to uniform choice and the exact-$\epsilon$ ladder on three panels with three training seeds, and carries no planning-ability or generality claim.
