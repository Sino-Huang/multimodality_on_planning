\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

% Paragraph purpose: Restate the primary contribution, a search-choice measurement validated before it scores policies, with one receipt per claim.
A search-choice measurement must be validated before it scores policies, and ours is. The identity audit returns both verdicts on one runtime, 48/48 identical pairs under sorted ties and 19/48 divergent pairs under submission order (Section~\ref{sec:results-identity}), and it admits the choice-frontier contract, whose decisions diverge.
% Evidence (moved from Results 5.2, already in the body): outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit.json pairs_identical 48 of 48; #134 outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit-submission-order.json rules.submission_order.pairs_divergent 19 of 48.
Its budget counts decisions, which applies the compute-matched-baseline principle \citep{li2019random}. Its M1 then passes the pre-registered ladder on the validation panel and again on the held-out panel (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}). A higher M1 therefore reads as choice closer to the reference's along the ladder's $\epsilon$ axis. The rungs read the reference's computed priority order, which is never shown to a policy, so the ladder validates the measurement, not a policy.

% Paragraph purpose: Give the development-stage answer to the title from the trained adapter's three-panel result.
The development-stage answer to the title is that a trained vision-language model (VLM) adapter learns to choose which frontier state to expand in the two additive best-first variants better than random-valid, without approaching the reference. Under the enumeration contract no BFS or BFWS cell shows learned success. The trained adapter's M1 exceeds random-valid's on every panel, descriptively on the unscreened panel (Section~\ref{sec:results-adapter}). Its M1 of 0.151 to 0.432 stays well below the reference's 0.875, which is fixed by construction.

% Paragraph purpose: State three separately evidenced practices for benchmark designers and policy trainers, pointing to Box 1.
Box~\ref{box:procedure} states three practices for benchmark designers and policy trainers. First, designers should audit decisions on the executed runtime and report the divergent fraction. Second, designers should count budgets in decisions or expansions whenever a zero-token control exists (Box~\ref{box:procedure}, step 2). Third, trainers should report the gain over random-valid with its ladder position, split by audit verdict. A gain over the base is not choice, as the seed-replication cell shows (Section~\ref{sec:introduction}). A gain over random-valid is not near-reference choice unless it separates from a rung.

% Paragraph purpose: List the limitations, one line each.
\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Ladder position.} The trained adapter is separated above the 0.75 rung only on the held-out panel, not on the validation panel, the unscreened panel, or pooled.
\item \emph{Ladder axis.} The ladder validates M1 only for mixtures of the reference with random choice, and a privileged selector that reaches the goal in fewer decisions scores above the reference.
\item \emph{Screening.} The held-out panel is screened like the validation panel, and on the unscreened panel the gain rests on 4 of 12 tasks.
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json concentration.per_panel.p2u.counts positive 4, zero 8; unscreened_p2u.ladder.verdict PASS (descriptive); P2u 0/10 random-valid screening goals on all 12 tasks: docs/experiments/choice-frontier/issue-139-closeout.md L56 (#139).
\item \emph{Seeds.} There are three training seeds, and seed 29 is weakest on every panel (descriptive).
% Evidence: seed 29 lowest per panel: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/seeds/metrics/analysis.json primary.per_seed.29; outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json heldout_p2.primary.per_seed.29, unscreened_p2u.primary.per_seed.29 (#138, #139).
\item \emph{Final evaluation.} The frozen 45-task final evaluation is unexecuted.
\item \emph{Prediction.} The pre-registered majority prediction for submission-order divergence failed.
\item \emph{Runtime.} The runtime never chooses or repairs operands, so no result shows the model searching without the runtime's validation.
\item \emph{External audit.} The LLM-First Search reference uses a substituted local model, and the audit covers 80 tasks from four configurations.
% Evidence: outputs/external-audit/v2/lfs/identity-audit.json tasks 80, per_config {cd3, cd5, cd7, su4}; configs/experiments/external-audit-v2/lfs-reference-server.json .model (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct) (#142).
\item \emph{Second policy.} The second policy scored is zero-shot, and the trained second backbone was not run under the choice-frontier contract (it ran at reduced scope under the enumeration contract). The measurement's ability to order two trained choosers that both beat random-valid is therefore untested.
\item \emph{Menu surface.} Our manipulations of the enumeration contract's action menu cannot separate surface-following from state grounding, which only the unrun test comparing fixed with random distractor positions would do.
\item \emph{Modality.} The choice-frontier results use one scene-only observation, so no modality is compared under that contract.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Paragraph purpose: Lead with the primary contribution, give the development-stage answer to the title in one adapter sentence, and close with the claim frame.
We validate a search-choice measurement before it scores any policy. An identity audit and a decision-counted budget admit the interface, and a pre-registered privileged ladder validates M1 on two panels. Scored this way, a trained adapter beats random-valid for frontier selection in the two additive best-first variants ($D_3 = +0.285$ $[+0.140, +0.433]$ on the validation panel), without approaching the reference. Separation above the 0.75 rung holds only on the held-out panel.
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v4/seeds/metrics/analysis.json primary.D3, primary.ci95 (#138); outputs/choice-frontier/v4/panels/metrics/analysis.json heldout_p2.separation.vs_eps_0.75.verdict (#139).
The choice-quality claim is development-stage, holds relative to random-valid and the exact-$\epsilon$ ladder on three panels with three training seeds, and carries no planning-ability or generality claim.
