\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

% Paragraph purpose: Interpret what the two-verdict audit and the ladder license, with pointers to the receipts.
Because the identity check returns both verdicts on one runtime (Section~\ref{sec:results-identity}), random-valid success separates choice from validity only after the check has been run on the interface at hand. The ladder licenses reading a higher M1 as better choice on the validation panel (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}). The rungs read the privileged heap head, so the ladder validates the ordering of the measurement, not any realisable policy.
Menu-manipulation and zoo results on the earlier contracts are in the Menu-Manipulation Receipts appendix (Appendix~\ref{app:results-menu}) and the Choice-Frontier and Comparator-Zoo Receipts appendix (Appendix~\ref{app:results-cf}).

% Paragraph purpose: Interpret the at-rung position of the learned result and state its boundaries, including the screened-panel and concentration caveat.
The scaled adapter's gain over uniform choice places it at the rung where three quarters of decisions are uniform (Section~\ref{sec:results-adapter}), so it chooses better than chance but far from the reference.
This is a development-stage, single-seed result on one screened panel, obtained by imitating the exact reference, and it supports no planning-ability, generality, or held-out claim.
Screening admitted only tasks where random-valid is discriminative, and the effect is concentrated, with 4 of the 12 tasks at or below zero on the per-task difference.

None of the seven published interfaces we surveyed uses a complete-set submission contract, so the identity failure is shown only on our runtime. The contribution is therefore the measurement, not the exposure of a common flaw.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Paragraph purpose: Give the scoped answer, the validated measurement and learned result at its rung, and the claim frame.
On our runtime a per-decision identity check returns both verdicts. The redesigned choice-frontier measurement passes a pre-registered ladder test, and a scaled adapter beats uniform choice with M1 at the exact-$\epsilon$ 0.75 rung on point estimates. The choice-quality claim is development-stage and single-seed, holds on the validation panel relative to uniform choice and the exact-$\epsilon$ ladder, and carries no planning-ability, generality, or held-out claim.
