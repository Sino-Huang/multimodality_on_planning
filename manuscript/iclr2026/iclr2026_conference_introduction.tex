\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph purpose: Establish classical planning as a controlled probe of foundation-model reasoning, without introducing this paper's method.
Classical planning requires finding a sequence of actions that transforms an initial state into a goal state under specified constraints, a search problem whose formal structure allows instances to be generated systematically, solutions verified automatically, and difficulty controlled precisely, making it a natural probe of reasoning in foundation models \citep{valmeekam2023critical,stein2025autoplanbench,kokel2026acpbenchhard}. The formal model behind each instance fixes the task semantics, so errors reflect reasoning rather than ambiguity in the problem statement. The explicit state space also supports two levels of question: how actions apply locally, and how states connect globally. These properties let an evaluator vary difficulty along controlled dimensions and check each answer mechanically.

% Paragraph purpose: Summarize the prior study's diagnosis of two non-compensatory competencies and its observational scope.
A recent study used this setting to measure latent planning competence in off-the-shelf language models \citep{huang2026twodistinct}. It separated two competencies that did not compensate for each other. Operational reasoning covers local applicability, progression, and validation: whether an action is legal in a state, what state it produces, and whether a goal condition holds. Structural enumeration covers goal reachability, landmarks, and other global state-space structure. Across model scales, and under chain-of-thought prompting, targeted prompting, and scratchpad or agent harnesses, operational reasoning improved, but these gains were not accompanied by comparable structural gains. The evidence was item-level and text-only, drawn from models used as released, without task-specific training.

% Paragraph purpose: Identify the gap left by that diagnosis and motivate direct training of search execution.
Structural enumeration is related to global search-space reasoning, but it is not identical to full search execution. Identifying which states are reachable, or which serve as landmarks, does not require controlling a frontier across a budgeted episode. The prior study established this profile observationally. It did not test whether the structural competency can be acquired through training, or whether a model can sustain a declared procedure over a complete episode. Closing that gap calls for direct intervention: train the model to make the exploration decisions of a classical search algorithm, then measure whether structural/process competence changes.

% Paragraph purpose: Reveal the Search Process Policy/Trusted Search Runtime mechanism and its replay-determinative ownership rule.
Our intervention replaces direct plan generation with learned execution of a declared search algorithm. A Search Process Policy (Section~\ref{sec:policy}) is trained to emit Typed Search Operations, discrete steps such as expanding a state, generating a successor, updating the frontier, or applying a goal test. A Trusted Search Runtime validates and applies each emission, checks Algorithm Invariants on every operation, and maintains bounded external Search Memory holding frontier, visited, novelty, and priority state. This division is replay-determinative: the emission must contain every operand that determines exploration, and the runtime may not select omitted operands, reorder candidates for the policy, or silently repair an invalid emission. Replaying the raw emissions against the same task and Search Memory must reproduce the explored trace. Operation validity, invariant compliance, and episode success are measured separately from learned control advantage, which is computed against an oracle-assisted, validity-constrained random-valid control that samples only runtime-valid operations.

% Paragraph purpose: State the research questions and study design, separating executed components from unmeasured ones.
The study asks whether a policy can learn to execute declared algorithms with valid, invariant-compliant traces, whether learned execution yields control advantage over the random-valid control, and whether the acquired capability transfers to planning benchmarks and broader search-intensive reasoning. The active algorithms are BFS, a complete unpruned width-based best-first search (BFWS), and two additive best-first variants, weighted A* (weight 3) and greedy best-first, both over $h_{\rm add}$. Process supervision on search-trace segments is the core training condition, and two-iteration DAgger, model-generated successors, curriculum-by-modality, observation-corruption stress tests, and failure-mechanism calibration have also been executed. Modality conditions present the same task content as text-state, visual-state, or multimodal-state observations under equal Search Memory capacity. References are the pretrained base, the random-valid control, and exact classical execution (Section~\ref{sec:design}). Reduced-scope generalization/robustness and second-backbone stages, and full-scope zero-shot transfer to FOLIO, GSM8K, and HumanEval, have been executed under frozen protocols. Training-seed variance has been measured for two prospective replication cells (seeds 17/29/71, descriptive $n{=}3$), with all other cells single-seed. % Paragraph purpose: Map the current evidence as a descriptive map of where learned control emerges, how it fails, and which observation channel carries it.
The results (Section~\ref{sec:results}) form a descriptive map of where learned control emerges, how it fails, and which observation channel carries it. On a frozen 15-task text-state development panel under BFWS, one fixed-seed process-SFT run achieved 0.933 success, versus 0.467 for random-valid, 0.0 for the base, and 1.0 for exact execution. It is the sole clear positive learned-control comparison in the study (the broader panels below return null or negative contrasts) and is development-stage.

% Paragraph purpose: Contrast the expanded baseline with the bounded BFWS finding and localize learned failures.
On the BFS and additive best-first development panels, process SFT matched random-valid at 100\%, so saturation left no room for measured advantage. An expanded baseline of 24 whole tasks held disjoint from all training rows across 12 domains and all three modalities sharpens the boundary: process SFT succeeded on 125 of 288 instances versus 240 for random-valid, 0 for the base, and 288 for exact execution, with every success in additive best-first cells, while BFS and width-based best-first failed on all 24 tasks in every modality. The development-panel BFWS positive does not reappear on this suite. A failure-mechanism calibration over 2,910 replay-verified episodes explains this pattern. The base fails immediately, predominantly on malformed outputs (379 malformed, 67 rejected frontier operations). The dominant learned failure is a parsed, source-correct, applicable operation rejected by the search invariant early in the episode. The random-valid control fails only late on expansion-budget exhaustion. Process supervision teaches operation generation, not invariant-consistent frontier intent.

% Paragraph purpose: Summarize executed generalization, training, and transfer extensions.
The executed extension arms bound this map. Learned adapters keep succeeding under 25 frozen semantics-preserving perturbation variants (128/150 versus 0/150 for the base, with the lossy name-compression stratum reported separately). Two DAgger iterations added no advantage over exposure-matched continued SFT (1/72 versus 1/72). Replacing trusted successors with model-generated ones dropped goal success from 45/45 to 1/45. Curriculum-by-modality showed no ordering-by-modality interaction, and zero-shot transfer to FOLIO, GSM8K, and HumanEval left no comparison surviving Holm correction (0/36).

% Paragraph purpose: Bound the second-backbone and observation-channel findings by their development-stage scope.
A reduced second-backbone replication with InternVL3.5-8B (which shares the Qwen3-8B LLM family, isolating the vision tower, connector, and multimodal recipe) reached no goal in any modality. Observation-corruption probes then localize the learned controller: against 0.917 clean success, masking or shuffling the language channel drops success by up to 0.944 while blanking or degrading the rendered states drops success by at most 0.111, and the text-minus-visual isolation contrast is material ($-0.694$, 95\% CI $[-0.889, -0.472]$). The learned search control runs on the textual search-memory scaffold, and rendered visual semantics are near-dispensable under the frozen observation contract. All of these findings are development-stage, with a single training seed per cell except two prospective replication cells (seeds 17/29/71, descriptive $n{=}3$) whose headline positive and DAgger null both replicate.

% Paragraph purpose: State the three contributions supported by the current development-stage evidence.
This paper makes three contributions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{From diagnosis to intervention.} We execute the training-based follow-up to a diagnosed structural bottleneck: rather than prompt around flat structural enumeration, we train models to execute declared search algorithms and map where learned control emerges.
    \item \textbf{A replay-determinative policy/runtime interface.} Typed Search Operations carry every exploration-determining operand, while the no-repair Trusted Search Runtime validates and applies them. Operation validity, invariant compliance, episode success, and learned control advantage remain separate measurements.
    \item \textbf{A descriptive map with failure and channel localization.} On the expanded baseline, learned success appears only in additive best-first cells, while the bounded BFWS development panel supplies the sole positive learned-control comparison. Learned failures localize early as source-correct, applicable operations rejected by search invariants rather than malformed outputs. Observation corruption shows the learned controller runs on the textual search scaffold rather than the rendered state. DAgger, model-generated successors, transfer, and second-backbone replication mark the negative boundaries. This is development-stage evidence, not final efficacy.
\end{itemize}
