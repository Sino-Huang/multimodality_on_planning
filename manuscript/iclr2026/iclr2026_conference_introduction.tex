\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph purpose: Set up the question for planning readers: fine-tuning targets the plan, classical planning is built on search algorithms, whether a model can learn to execute them from their traces is open, and the question extends to VLMs.
The planning community often probes language models by fine-tuning them on planning problems, and the training target is usually the plan, the action sequence a planner returns, rather than the search that found it \citep{pallagani2022plansformer,rossetti2024learning,bohnet2024exploring,belcamino2026generalization}.
Yet classical planning is built on search algorithms, from blind breadth-first search (BFS) to best-first width search (BFWS) \citep{lipovetzky2017bestfirst} and heuristic best-first search \citep{hart1968formal,bonet2001planning}.
Models have been trained on A* traces in mazes, Sokoban, and Blocksworld \citep{lehnert2024beyond,saha2024system1x}, but how well a model learns to execute classical planning algorithms from their search traces is still open.
The question extends to vision-language models (VLMs), because embodied agents increasingly plan from what they see \citep{merler2025viplan,xu2025visual,hao2025simulation}.
% Sources: plan-target fine-tuning and trace-training credits follow Section 2 (related_work.tex ¶1-2 source comments); VLM planners over images per Section 2 ¶4.

% Paragraph purpose: State what we do in plain terms: which model, algorithms, domains, and observations; the model executes every search step; the runtime checks each step and stops at the first invalid one, so success means a valid, goal-reaching search; and what random-valid is.
We fine-tune low-rank adapters \citep{hu2022lora} for Qwen3-VL-8B \citep{bai2025qwen3vl} on the search traces of four algorithms: BFS, BFWS, and greedy and weighted-A* best-first search with the additive heuristic $h_{\rm add}$ \citep{bonet2001planning}.
The tasks come from 12 PDDL domains, and each state is given as a text, visual (image), or multimodal (combined) observation.
At each step, a runtime lists as text the applicable actions, the resulting states, and facts such as visited flags and BFWS novelty values. The model reads them and names the state to expand, the successors to add, and any priority values the algorithm needs.
Each step is one training record, so a trace can be much longer than its plan: on the ferry task of Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}a, the 4-action plan takes 11 greedy best-first operations.
% Evidence (Fig. 1a ferry example, 4-action plan vs 11 greedy best-first operations, one step per record): teaser specification from Main (2026-09-26); figures/fig_teaser.py must assert both counts when the Figures agent regenerates panel (a).
The runtime keeps the frontier, checks each step against the declared algorithm's rules, and ends the episode at the first step that breaks them.
A solved episode is therefore a valid run of the declared search that reaches the goal (Section~\ref{sec:policy}).
Our main control, random-valid, picks uniformly among valid steps. Because the runtime supplies the candidates and orders the frontier, it is an oracle-assisted bound on validity, not a competing learner (Section~\ref{sec:design}).

% Evidence: runtime-supplied grounded candidates as text in every observation type, and random-valid as an oracle-assisted valid-operation control: synthesis-v1/README.md claim baseline-controls and "Claim boundaries"; appendix text-scaffold caveat (drafts/pre_storyline_v2/iclr2026_conference_appendix.tex L517).

% Paragraph purpose: F1. All four algorithms are learnable at very different data costs; give the 512-record grid, the large-corpus development runs with their unmatched-panel qualifier, and the second-backbone replication.
All four algorithms are learnable, but their data costs differ widely (Section~\ref{sec:results-data-cost}, Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}b).
With 512 training records per adapter, the two additive variants solve 125 of 144 episodes (24 tasks, three observation types), while BFS and BFWS solve none (Table~\ref{tab:results-main}).
% Evidence: docs/experiments/expanded-study/synthesis-v1/baseline-summary.csv process_sft rows: additive successes 22+20+22+18+21+22 = 125 of 6 x 24 = 144; bfs and best_first_width 0/24 in every modality. 512 records, one epoch, 16 updates, seed 17: synthesis-v1/README.md and outputs/expanded-study/v1/seed-replication/analysis.json.
Larger text-observation corpora make both learnable.
With 12,994 records, BFS reaches 1.0 success on a 15-task development panel, on which random-valid also reaches 1.0.
With 47,780 records, BFWS reaches 0.933 on another 15-task panel, against 0.467 for random-valid.
These runs use separate, smaller development panels, so this is not a matched-size comparison.

A second backbone, InternVL3.5-8B \citep{wang2025internvl35}, shows the same split at 512 records, solving 31 of 36 greedy best-first episodes and no BFS episode.

% Paragraph purpose: F2. The learned execution rests on the text channel; give the corruption contrasts, and scope the finding to the textual candidate list the runtime supplies.
Across observation types, the learned execution rests on the text (Section~\ref{sec:results-modality}, Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}c).
For the additive adapters, shuffling the text lowers success by 0.944 with text and 0.722 with multimodal observations.
Blanking or degrading the images lowers it by at most 0.111, and the intervals for these image corruptions all include zero.
% Evidence: outputs/expanded-study/v1/modality-stress/analysis.json contrasts by family x arm (text-shuffled text -0.944 [-1.000, -0.833], text-shuffled multimodal -0.722 [-0.889, -0.500], visual-blank and visual-degraded -0.056 / -0.111 with upper endpoints +0.000); docs/experiments/expanded-study/issue-126-closeout.md; appendix table tab:results-corruption.
Because the candidate list stays textual in every observation type, this shows that the trained models read the textual serialization, not that images are useless in principle.

% Paragraph purpose: F3. The learned heuristic search holds up under task variations, scoped to the two additive adapters with saturated controls, and juxtaposed (not compared) with reported plan-training sensitivity.
The learned heuristic search also holds up under task variations (Section~\ref{sec:results-robustness}).
We tested the two additive adapters on 25 variant tasks from five families. They succeed on 128 of 150 episodes, the pretrained base on none, and random-valid and the exact reference (the declared algorithm run by the runtime) on all.
Success stays between 26 and 29 of 30 under object renaming, name compression, image restyling, and scale-up, and falls to 19 of 30 under shifted initial states.
% Evidence: docs/experiments/expanded-study/synthesis-v1/README.md §6 and claim generalization-v2-headline (learned 128/150, base 0/150, random_valid 750/750, exact_reference 150/150; object-renaming 29/30, name-compression 27/30, render-restyle 27/30, scale-up 26/30, shifted-init 19/30).
\citet{belcamino2026generalization} report significant drops for a plan-trained model under symbol anonymization and compact plan serialization.
Their model writes whole plans, ours emits single search steps over runtime-supplied candidates, and we ran no plan-trained baseline, so the results are juxtaposed, not compared.
% Sources: arXiv:2601.14456 abstract ("Symbol anonymization and compact serialization cause significant performance drops despite preserving plan semantics"), checked 2026-09-26.

% Paragraph purpose: F4. BFWS-trace adapters raise FOLIO accuracy under all three observations; state that the gain is not significant after Holm correction and that these adapters solve no planning task at 512 records.
Training on BFWS traces also transfers beyond planning (Section~\ref{sec:results-transfer}).
The BFWS adapters raise FOLIO logical-reasoning accuracy \citep{han2022folio} from 0.600 to 0.660, 0.645, and 0.645 with text, visual, and multimodal observations, and no adapter moves GSM8K or HumanEval accuracy \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k,chen2021humaneval} by more than 0.04.
The gain is consistent but not statistically significant, since no transfer comparison survives Holm correction over 36 comparisons \citep{holm1979simple}.
These are the 512-record adapters, so the BFWS adapter that improves FOLIO solves none of the 24 planning tasks.

% Paragraph purpose: State the three contributions, each a bold lead phrase and one plain sentence.
We make three contributions.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,parsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{An empirical study of search-trace supervision for VLMs.} We fine-tune a VLM on traces of four classical search algorithms over 12 PDDL domains and three observation types, scoring only fully valid searches.
    \item \textbf{Data-cost and observation-channel findings.} The additive variants are learned from 512 records, BFS and BFWS need over ten times more, and the learned execution depends on the text in the observation.
    \item \textbf{Evidence on robustness and transfer.} The learned heuristic search withstands renaming, restyling, and scale-up, and BFWS traces give a consistent but non-significant FOLIO gain.
\end{itemize}
