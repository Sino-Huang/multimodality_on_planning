\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph purpose: Establish classical planning as a controlled probe of foundation-model reasoning, without introducing this paper's method.
Classical planning requires finding a sequence of actions that transforms an initial state into a goal state under specified constraints, a search problem whose formal structure allows instances to be generated systematically, solutions verified automatically, and difficulty controlled precisely, making it a natural probe of reasoning in foundation models \citep{valmeekam2023critical,stein2025autoplanbench,kokel2026acpbenchhard}. The formal model behind each instance fixes the task semantics, so errors reflect reasoning rather than ambiguity in the problem statement. The explicit state space also supports two levels of question: how actions apply locally, and how states connect globally. These properties let an evaluator vary difficulty along controlled dimensions and check each answer mechanically.

% Paragraph purpose: Summarize the prior study's diagnosis of two non-compensatory competencies and its observational scope.
A recent study used this setting to measure latent planning competence in off-the-shelf language models \citep{huang2026twodistinct}. It separated two competencies that did not compensate for each other. Operational reasoning covers local applicability, progression, and validation: whether an action is legal in a state, what state it produces, and whether a goal condition holds. Structural enumeration covers goal reachability, landmarks, and other global state-space structure. Across model scales, and under chain-of-thought prompting, targeted prompting, and scratchpad or agent harnesses, operational reasoning improved, but these gains were not accompanied by comparable structural gains. The evidence was item-level and text-only, drawn from models used as released, without task-specific training.

% Paragraph purpose: Identify the gap left by that diagnosis and motivate direct training of search execution.
Structural enumeration is related to global search-space reasoning, but it is not identical to full search execution. Identifying which states are reachable, or which serve as landmarks, does not require controlling a frontier across a budgeted episode. The prior study established this profile observationally. It did not test whether the structural competency can be acquired through training, or whether a model can sustain a declared procedure over a complete episode. Closing that gap calls for direct intervention: train the model to make the exploration decisions of a classical search algorithm, then measure whether structural/process competence changes.

% Paragraph purpose: Reveal the Search Process Policy/Trusted Search Runtime mechanism and its replay-determinative ownership rule.
Our intervention replaces direct plan generation with learned execution of a declared search algorithm. A Search Process Policy (Section~\ref{sec:policy}) is trained to emit Typed Search Operations, discrete steps such as expanding a state, generating a successor, updating the frontier, or applying a goal test. A Trusted Search Runtime validates and applies each emission, checks Algorithm Invariants on every operation, and maintains bounded external Search Memory holding frontier, visited, novelty, and priority state. This division is replay-determinative: the emission must contain every operand that determines exploration, and the runtime may not select omitted operands, reorder candidates for the policy, or silently repair an invalid emission. Replaying the raw emissions against the same task and Search Memory must reproduce the explored trace. Operation validity, invariant compliance, and episode success are measured separately from learned control advantage, which is computed against an oracle-assisted, validity-constrained random-valid control that samples only runtime-valid operations.

% Paragraph purpose: State the research questions and study design, separating executed components from unmeasured ones.
The study asks whether a policy can learn to execute declared algorithms with valid, invariant-compliant traces, whether learned execution yields control advantage over the random-valid control, and whether the acquired capability transfers to planning benchmarks and broader search-intensive reasoning. The active algorithms are BFS, a complete unpruned width-based best-first search (BFWS), and two additive best-first variants, weighted A* (weight 3) and greedy best-first, both over $h_{\rm add}$. Process supervision on search-trace segments is the core training condition, and two-iteration DAgger, model-generated successors, curriculum-by-modality, observation-corruption stress tests, and failure-mechanism calibration have also been executed. Modality conditions present the same task content as text-state, visual-state, or multimodal-state observations under equal Search Memory capacity. References are the pretrained base, the random-valid control, and exact classical execution (Section~\ref{sec:design}). Reduced-scope generalization/robustness and second-backbone stages, and full-scope zero-shot transfer to FOLIO, GSM8K, and HumanEval, have been executed under frozen protocols. Training-seed variance has been measured for two prospective replication cells (seeds 17/29/71, descriptive $n{=}3$), with all other cells single-seed. Follow-up studies added image-only observation arms, menu manipulation, a per-decision identity audit, and a redesigned choice-frontier contract with a comparator zoo. The held-out final primary evaluation has not been executed, so no final comparative efficacy finding exists.

% Paragraph purpose: Lead the results map with the enumeration-contract identity finding and the menu-manipulation measurement that together show choice quality was unmeasurable.
The results (Section~\ref{sec:results}) begin with a measurement finding about the original enumeration contract, in which the policy picks each operation from a grounded, unscored candidate menu while textual Search Memory carries frontier and score state. A per-decision identity audit compares the random-valid control against exact reference on every additive cell of the expanded baseline and finds them decision- and expansion-identical on 48/48 additive pairs. Choice quality is therefore unmeasurable by construction on these cells, and saturating random-valid entries measure operation validity. Menu manipulation then measures what the trained policy reads. Permuting the menu leaves 36/36 successes with 0/512 invalid operations, while injecting schema-valid but scene-inapplicable distractors collapses success to 0/36, with 36/60 invalid operations and a distractor pick-rate of 0.6. The policy reads menu content without grounding applicability in the scene.

Image-only native arms point the same way. With textual search memory and score scalars removed and the grounded menu retained, both arms succeed on 18/18 with zero invalid operations, against 16/18 for the published frozen \texttt{visual-state} arm, while random-valid sits at ceiling, so no structural-advantage claim follows. Blanking or degrading every image leaves the history arm unchanged ($0.000$ $[0.000, 0.000]$), which the closeout reads as a menu leak. The decision-relevant signal is the candidate menu itself.

% Paragraph purpose: Present the audit-driven choice-frontier redesign, its pre-registered identity gate, the first measurement, and the comparator-zoo calibration with its non-equivalence boundary.
These findings motivate a redesign. Under the choice-frontier contract the policy selects which runtime-owned frontier state to expand next under a binding decision budget of 2$\times$ the additive exact-reference expansion count. Its own identity audit was a pre-registered pass condition, and it passes with 18/18 control pairs divergent. Headroom becomes measurable (random-valid minus exact reference $-0.644$, 95\% CI $[-0.856, -0.422]$). In this first measurement the learned adapter solves 7/18 cells with zero invalid operations, a contract-fluency gain of $+0.389$ $[+0.167, +0.611]$ over the pretrained base. Its contrast against random-valid is $+0.033$ $[-0.033, +0.133]$, not material and driven by a single task. An exploratory comparator zoo places the adapter's solve-versus-budget AUC (.1042) below novelty-first selection (.1250) and random-valid (.1389), and a fifteen-test Holm family finds no positive learned Pareto dominance. The selectors read privileged information the model does not see. Non-significance here is neither equivalence nor a generalized negative capability claim, since no equivalence margin was pre-registered.

% Paragraph purpose: Place the enumeration-contract development map under the audit, with the demoted BFWS contrast, failure localization, extensions, cross-backbone replication, and channel localization.
The enumeration-contract development map is read in this light. One positive contrast exists, on a frozen 15-task text-state BFWS development panel, where one fixed-seed process-SFT run achieved 0.933 success versus 0.467 for random-valid, 0.0 for the base, and 1.0 for exact execution. On an expanded baseline of 24 whole tasks across 12 domains and all three modalities, process SFT succeeded on 125 of 288 instances versus 240 for random-valid, 0 for the base, and 288 for exact execution, with every success in additive best-first cells. A failure-mechanism calibration over 2,910 replay-verified episodes finds the dominant learned failure to be a source-correct, applicable operation rejected by the search invariant early in the episode, while the random-valid control fails only late on expansion-budget exhaustion. Observation corruption localizes the learned controller in the language channel. Masking or shuffling it drops success by up to 0.944, while blanking or degrading rendered states drops success by at most 0.111. A second-backbone cell with InternVL3.5-8B under greedy additive best-first reaches 31/36 against 0/36 for the base, so the additive-family result replicates across backbones, and the earlier BFS null on that backbone is an algorithm confound. The extension arms bound the map further. DAgger added no advantage over exposure-matched continued SFT (1/72 versus 1/72), model-generated successors dropped goal success from 45/45 to 1/45, and zero-shot transfer to FOLIO, GSM8K, and HumanEval left no comparison surviving Holm correction (0/36). All of these findings are development-stage, with a single training seed per cell except two prospective replication cells (seeds 17/29/71, descriptive $n{=}3$).

% Paragraph purpose: State the three contributions supported by the current development-stage evidence under the measurement framing.
This paper makes three contributions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A measurability audit for grounded-menu search evaluation.} We show that under the enumeration contract a grounded action menu makes search-choice quality unmeasurable, and we operationalize the audit as two cheap checks any grounded-menu evaluation can run: the per-decision identity test of random-valid against the reference, and menu manipulation by permutation and distractor injection.
    \item \textbf{An audit-driven choice-frontier contract.} Built on a replay-determinative policy/runtime interface with a no-repair Trusted Search Runtime, the redesigned contract makes the policy choose which frontier state to expand under a binding budget. Its identity audit was a pre-registered pass condition, and it passes, so choice headroom becomes measurable.
    \item \textbf{A development-stage map with its negative boundaries.} The first choice-frontier measurement shows contract fluency without demonstrated choice quality beyond the named references, and a comparator zoo places the adapter below named selectors. Under the enumeration contract one positive BFWS contrast exists. Learned failures localize to invariant rejections of applicable operations, and the learned controller runs on the textual scaffold rather than the rendered state. DAgger adds no advantage, model-generated successors collapse goal success, and transfer leaves no comparison surviving correction. This is development-stage evidence, not final efficacy.
\end{itemize}
