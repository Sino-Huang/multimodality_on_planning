\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph purpose: Pose the title question as a measurement problem and set up the policy, runtime, algorithms, observations, and the natural control.
To ask whether a vision-language model can learn to execute a classical search algorithm, we need a success measure that responds to the model's choices.
Prior probes evaluate language models on classical planning \citep{valmeekam2023critical,stein2025autoplanbench,kokel2026acpbenchhard}.
We instead train a Search Process Policy to emit Typed Search Operations, which a no-repair Trusted Search Runtime validates (Section~\ref{sec:policy}).
The declared algorithms are breadth-first search (BFS), Best-First Width Search (BFWS), and additive best-first search, which is best-first search ordered by the additive heuristic $h_{\rm add}$.
The policy acts under one of two decision contracts, with text, visual, or multimodal observations under the first and scene-only observations under the second.
The natural control is random-valid, which chooses uniformly over valid operations.

% Paragraph purpose: Show the stakes, namely that random-valid can reproduce the reference so that every process-SFT success measures validity.
Random-valid can make every decision the reference makes, and then a learned gain is validity, not choice.
Under our enumeration contract, each expansion submits its complete candidate set.
In its additive best-first cells, random-valid makes exactly the decisions of the exact reference, the declared algorithm run as specified, on 48/48 pairs (Figure~\ref{fig:contracts}a).

% Paragraph purpose: State the primary contribution, namely the admitted choice-frontier measurement and the ladder that validates it before any policy is scored, with the privileged-rung boundary.
We therefore validate the measurement before scoring any policy, starting from a choice-frontier contract in which the policy picks the runtime-owned frontier state to expand.
It passes the identity audit with 18/18 divergent pairs (Figure~\ref{fig:contracts}b), and its budget counts decisions, twice the reference's expansion count, applying the compute-matched-baseline principle \citep{li2019random}.
M1, the area under the solve-versus-budget curve, must then pass a pre-registered ladder of exact-$\epsilon$ selectors, which randomize a fraction $\epsilon$ of reference decisions.
On the validation panel, 12 fresh tasks from 7 domains, the ladder passes with smallest adjacent gap $+0.082$ $[+0.051, +0.111]$.

% Paragraph purpose: State the three contributions without numbers, the validate-before-score measurement first; receipts are in the paragraphs above.
This paper makes three contributions.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,parsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{A validate-before-score search-choice measurement, admitted by the identity audit and a decision-counted budget, whose M1 passes a pre-registered privileged ladder on validation and held-out panels before any policy is scored.}
    \item \textbf{Evidence that the admission step is needed. On one runtime the identity audit returns both verdicts from a tie-break change alone, and it locates every process-SFT success on the expanded baseline in cells where success measures validity.}
    \item \textbf{A worked use and a portable procedure (Box~\ref{box:procedure}). The measurement scores a trained vision-language adapter above random-valid on three panels and untrained policies at its level, and the procedure is applied to two published interfaces, crediting its budget step to the compute-matched-baseline principle \citep{li2019random}.}
\end{itemize}
