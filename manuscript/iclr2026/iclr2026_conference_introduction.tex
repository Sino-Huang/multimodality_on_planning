\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph purpose: Motivate the question of separating choice from operation validity for trained search policies, and state the policy/runtime mechanism and scope.
On classical planning probes \citep{valmeekam2023critical,stein2025autoplanbench,kokel2026acpbenchhard}, off-the-shelf language models improve at operational reasoning with scale and prompting but not at structural enumeration \citep{huang2026twodistinct}, leaving open whether a model can be trained to sustain a declared search procedure. We train a Search Process Policy (Section~\ref{sec:policy}) to emit Typed Search Operations that a no-repair Trusted Search Runtime validates against Algorithm Invariants, over BFS, width-based, and additive best-first search under text, visual, and multimodal observations (Section~\ref{sec:design}). Success on such an interface can reflect operation validity or choice among valid operations. A random-valid control, which picks uniformly among valid operations, separates the two only when its decisions can differ from the reference's, so we ask whether search choice can be measured separately from operation validity.

% Paragraph purpose: Give the identity-audit receipts with both verdicts on the same runtime, then the redesigned contract and its validated measurement with the privileged-rung boundary.
On the additive best-first cells of our enumeration contract, where an expansion submits its complete candidate set and heap serials follow the sorted candidate order, random-valid is decision-identical to the exact reference on 48/48 pairs, so success there measures operation validity rather than choice.

On the same runtime with heap serials assigned in submission order, the same check finds 19/48 divergent pairs, so the check can return either verdict. The pre-registered prediction of divergence on most pairs (more than 24/48) did not hold (Section~\ref{sec:results-identity}).
% Evidence: outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit-submission-order.json rules.submission_order.pairs_divergent 19; preregistered_prediction.held false; threshold > 24/48 at preregistered_prediction.variant_diverges_in_most_pairs (#134 E134-02, E134-04, E134-07).
The redesigned choice-frontier contract (Figure~\ref{fig:contracts}) makes the policy select which runtime-owned frontier state to expand under a binding budget (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}).
On a fresh 12-task panel from 7 domains, the pre-registered ladder test passes. M1 orders the exact reference (0.875) above exact-$\epsilon$ selectors that choose uniformly on 25\% (0.793), 50\% (0.603), and 75\% (0.347) of decisions, and random-valid (0.021) lowest, and every adjacent paired task-clustered 95\% lower bound is positive (smallest gap $+0.082$ $[+0.051, +0.111]$).
% Evidence: configs/experiments/choice-frontier-v2/membership.json task_ids (12), domains (7); outputs/choice-frontier/v2/metrics/analysis.json instrument_validity.verdict PASS, arms.<arm>.m1_auc, instrument_validity.pairs[0].difference and .ci95 (#135 E135-01..16, E135-63).
The rungs read the privileged heap head, so the ladder validates the ordering of the measurement, not any realisable policy.

A scaled adapter beats uniform choice on this panel, $D = +0.328$ $[+0.155, +0.508]$, which meets the pre-registered POSITIVE rule for its single training seed. Its M1 of 0.349 sits at the exact-$\epsilon$ 0.75 rung (0.347) on point estimates, far below the 0.50 rung (0.603) (Section~\ref{sec:results-adapter}).

This is a development-stage, single-seed result on one screened panel, obtained by imitating the exact reference, and it supports no planning-ability, generality, or held-out claim.
On ScienceWorld \citep{wang2022scienceworld}, a published sequential-choice interface, random-valid diverges from the gold trajectory at the first decision in 1000/1000 pairs, with success 0.915 versus 0 for random-valid over 200 tasks, so success there measures choice. The audit covers 6 of its 30 task types, and none of the seven published interfaces we surveyed uses a complete-set submission contract, so the identity failure is shown only on our runtime (Section~\ref{sec:results-external}).

% Paragraph purpose: State the four contributions supported by the current development-stage evidence.
This paper makes four contributions.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,parsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{A per-decision identity audit that returns both verdicts.} On one runtime the check finds random-valid identical to the reference on 48/48 pairs under sorted-order serials and divergent on 19/48 pairs under submission-order serials.
% Evidence: outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit-submission-order.json rules.sorted_candidate_order.pairs_identical 48, rules.submission_order.pairs_divergent 19 (#134 E134-03, E134-04).
    \item \textbf{A validated choice-frontier measurement.} Its M1 metric passes a pre-registered exact-$\epsilon$ ladder test on a fresh panel.
    \item \textbf{A first learned result above uniform choice, with its boundaries.} The scaled adapter meets the pre-registered rule for one training seed on one screened panel, at the exact-$\epsilon$ 0.75 rung.
    \item \textbf{An external anchor.} On ScienceWorld success measures choice, and a survey of seven published interfaces finds none with a complete-set submission contract.
\end{itemize}
