\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph purpose: Establish classical planning as a controlled probe of foundation-model reasoning, without introducing this paper's method.
Classical planning requires finding a sequence of actions that transforms an initial state into a goal state under specified constraints, a search problem whose formal structure allows instances to be generated systematically, solutions verified automatically, and difficulty controlled precisely, making it a natural probe of reasoning in foundation models \citep{valmeekam2023critical,stein2025autoplanbench,kokel2026acpbenchhard}. The formal model behind each instance fixes the task semantics, so errors reflect reasoning rather than ambiguity in the problem statement. The explicit state space also supports two levels of question: how actions apply locally, and how states connect globally. These properties let an evaluator vary difficulty along controlled dimensions and check each answer mechanically.

% Paragraph purpose: Summarize the prior study's diagnosis of two non-compensatory competencies and its observational scope.
A recent study used this setting to measure latent planning competence in off-the-shelf language models \citep{huang2026twodistinct}. It separated two competencies that did not compensate for each other. Operational reasoning covers local applicability, progression, and validation: whether an action is legal in a state, what state it produces, and whether a goal condition holds. Structural enumeration covers goal reachability, landmarks, and other global state-space structure. Across model scales, and under chain-of-thought prompting, targeted prompting, and scratchpad or agent harnesses, operational reasoning improved, but these gains were not accompanied by comparable structural gains. The evidence was item-level and text-only, drawn from models used as released, without task-specific training.

% Paragraph purpose: Identify the gap left by that diagnosis and motivate direct training of search execution.
Structural enumeration is related to global search-space reasoning, but it is not identical to full search execution. Identifying which states are reachable, or which serve as landmarks, does not require controlling a frontier across a budgeted episode. The prior study established this profile observationally. It did not test whether the structural competency can be acquired through training, or whether a model can sustain a declared procedure over a complete episode. Closing that gap calls for direct intervention: train the model to make the exploration decisions of a classical search algorithm, then measure whether structural/process competence changes.

% Paragraph purpose: Reveal the Search Process Policy/Trusted Search Runtime mechanism and its replay-determinative ownership rule.
Our intervention replaces direct plan generation with learned execution of a declared search algorithm. A Search Process Policy (Section~\ref{sec:policy}) is trained to emit Typed Search Operations, discrete steps such as expanding a state, generating a successor, updating the frontier, or applying a goal test. A Trusted Search Runtime validates and applies each emission, checks Algorithm Invariants on every operation, and maintains bounded external Search Memory holding frontier, visited, novelty, and priority state. This division is replay-determinative: the emission must contain every operand that determines exploration, and the runtime may not select omitted operands, reorder candidates for the policy, or silently repair an invalid emission. Replaying the raw emissions against the same task and Search Memory must reproduce the explored trace. Operation validity, invariant compliance, and episode success are measured separately from learned control advantage, which is computed against an oracle-assisted, validity-constrained random-valid control that samples only runtime-valid operations.

% Paragraph purpose: State the research questions and study design, separating executed components from unmeasured ones.
The study asks whether a policy can learn to execute declared algorithms with valid, invariant-compliant traces, whether learned execution yields control advantage over the random-valid control, and whether the acquired capability transfers to planning benchmarks and broader search-intensive reasoning. The active algorithms are BFS, a complete unpruned width-based best-first search (BFWS), and two additive best-first variants, weighted A* (weight 3) and greedy best-first, both over $h_{\rm add}$. Process supervision on search-trace segments is the core training condition, and two-iteration DAgger, model-generated successors, curriculum-by-modality, observation-corruption stress tests, and failure-mechanism calibration have also been executed. Modality conditions present the same task content as text-state, visual-state, or multimodal-state observations under equal Search Memory capacity. References are the pretrained base, the random-valid control, and exact classical execution (Section~\ref{sec:design}). Reduced-scope generalization/robustness and second-backbone stages, and full-scope zero-shot transfer to FOLIO, GSM8K, and HumanEval, have been executed under frozen protocols. Training-seed variance has been measured for two prospective replication cells (seeds 17/29/71, descriptive $n{=}3$), with all other cells single-seed. Follow-up studies added image-only observation arms, menu manipulation, a per-decision identity audit, and a redesigned choice-frontier contract with a comparator zoo. The held-out final primary evaluation has not been executed, so no final comparative efficacy finding exists.

% Paragraph purpose: Lead the results map with the identity finding scoped to the additive best-first cells, its submission-order cause, the choice-registering BFS/BFWS cells, and the menu-manipulation receipts at their licensed strength.
The results (Section~\ref{sec:results}) begin with a measurement finding about the original enumeration contract, in which the policy picks each operation from a grounded, unscored candidate menu while textual Search Memory carries frontier and score state. A per-decision identity audit compares the random-valid control against exact reference on every additive best-first cell of the expanded baseline and finds them decision- and expansion-identical on 48/48 pairs. The cause is submission-order invariance. An expansion must submit its complete candidate set, and heap order ignores the order of submission. Choice quality is therefore unmeasurable by construction on these cells, and saturating random-valid entries there measure operation validity. The BFS and width-based best-first cells of the same contract do register choice (random-valid 15/24 and 17/24 versus exact 24/24), and the learned policy scores 0/24 on each. Menu permutation leaves behavior intact (36/36 successes, 0/512 invalid operations), which is uninformative about order under submission-order invariance. Distractor injection collapses success to 0/36 at a distractor pick-rate of 0.6, against a chance rate of 0.592 to 0.625 over the same 60 decisions. The policy does not reject schema-valid inapplicable menu entries, with the pick-rate at chance.

% Paragraph purpose: Present the audit-driven choice-frontier redesign, its identity gate as a sanity check, the first measurement, and the comparator-zoo calibration with its non-equivalence boundary.
These findings motivate a redesign. Under the choice-frontier contract the policy selects which runtime-owned frontier state to expand next under a binding decision budget of 2$\times$ the additive exact-reference expansion count. Its identity audit was a pre-registered pass condition and is a sanity check that holds by construction whenever a menu offers two or more states. It passes on 9/9 tasks (18 cells, control sequences coincide across the two algorithms). Random-valid and exact separate (random-valid minus exact reference $-0.644$, frozen 95\% CI $[-0.856, -0.422]$, post-hoc task-clustered $[-0.889, -0.333]$). In this first measurement the learned adapter solves 7/18 cells with zero invalid operations, a contract-fluency gain of $+0.389$ $[+0.167, +0.611]$ over the pretrained base. Its contrast against random-valid is $+0.033$ $[-0.033, +0.133]$, not material. Policy-level resolution currently rests on one discriminative task. An exploratory comparator zoo finds no positive learned Pareto dominance under a fifteen-test Holm family. On the solve-versus-budget AUC the adapter's point estimate (.1042) lies between \texttt{bfs-order} (.0694) and novelty-first selection (.1250), with overlapping intervals, and random-valid (.1389) is the floor reference rather than a selector. The selectors read privileged information the model does not see. Non-significance here is neither equivalence nor a generalized negative capability claim, since no equivalence margin was pre-registered.

% Paragraph purpose: Place the enumeration-contract development map under the audit, with the single positive contrast, failure localization, extensions, cross-backbone replication of contract fluency, and the shuffled-only channel finding.
The enumeration-contract development map is read in this light. One positive learned contrast exists, on a frozen 15-task text-state BFWS development panel, where one fixed-seed process-SFT run achieved 0.933 success versus 0.467 for random-valid, 0.0 for the base, and 1.0 for exact execution. On an expanded baseline of 24 whole tasks across 12 domains and all three modalities, process SFT succeeded on 125 of 288 instances versus 240 for random-valid, 0 for the base, and 288 for exact execution, with every success in additive best-first cells, where the identity audit shows no decision to make. A failure-mechanism calibration over 2,910 replay-verified episodes finds the dominant learned failure to be a source-correct, applicable operation rejected by the search invariant early in the episode, while the random-valid control fails only late on expansion-budget exhaustion. Copyability-preserving text shuffling lowers success by $-0.944$ (text) and $-0.722$ (multimodal), while visual corruption costs at most $-0.111$. A second-backbone cell with InternVL3.5-8B under greedy additive best-first reaches 31/36 against 0/36 for the base, below random-valid at 24/24 per modality. That cell replicates contract fluency on the additive cells, and the earlier 0/36 BFS result on that backbone is consistent with an algorithm (choice-requirement) difference rather than a backbone null. The extension arms bound the map further. DAgger added no advantage over exposure-matched continued SFT (1/72 versus 1/72), model-generated successors dropped goal success from 45/45 to 1/45, and zero-shot transfer to FOLIO, GSM8K, and HumanEval left no comparison surviving Holm correction (0/36). All of these findings are development-stage, with a single training seed per cell except two prospective replication cells (seeds 17/29/71, descriptive $n{=}3$).

% Paragraph purpose: State the three contributions supported by the current development-stage evidence under the measurement framing.
This paper makes three contributions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A measurability audit for grounded-menu search evaluation.} We show that on the additive best-first cells of the enumeration contract, submission-order invariance makes random-valid decision-identical to the reference (48/48 pairs), so search-choice quality is unmeasurable there, while the BFS and width-based best-first cells of the same contract register choice. We operationalize the audit as two cheap checks, the per-decision identity test of random-valid against the reference and menu manipulation by permutation and distractor injection.
    \item \textbf{An audit-driven choice-frontier contract.} Built on a replay-determinative policy/runtime interface with a no-repair Trusted Search Runtime, the redesigned contract makes the policy choose which frontier state to expand under a binding budget. Its pre-registered identity gate, a sanity check that holds by construction whenever a menu offers two or more states, passes on 9/9 tasks (18 cells, control sequences coincide across the two algorithms), and random-valid and exact separate. Policy-level resolution currently rests on one discriminative task.
    \item \textbf{A development-stage map with its negative boundaries.} The first choice-frontier measurement shows contract fluency without demonstrated choice quality beyond the named references. A comparator zoo finds no positive learned Pareto dominance, and on M1 the adapter's point estimate (.1042) lies between \texttt{bfs-order} (.0694) and novelty-first selection (.1250), with overlapping intervals. Under the enumeration contract one positive learned contrast exists, on the 15-task width-based best-first development panel. Learned failures localize to invariant rejections of applicable operations, and copyability-preserving text shuffling lowers learned success far more than visual corruption. DAgger adds no advantage, model-generated successors collapse goal success, and transfer leaves no comparison surviving correction. This is development-stage evidence, not final efficacy.
\end{itemize}
