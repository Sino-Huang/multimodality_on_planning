\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph purpose: Motivate the question of separating choice from operation validity for trained search policies, and state the policy/runtime mechanism and scope.
On classical planning probes \citep{valmeekam2023critical,stein2025autoplanbench,kokel2026acpbenchhard}, off-the-shelf language models improve at operational reasoning with scale and prompting but not at structural enumeration \citep{huang2026twodistinct}, leaving open whether a model can be trained to sustain a declared search procedure. We train a Search Process Policy (Section~\ref{sec:policy}) to emit Typed Search Operations that a no-repair Trusted Search Runtime validates against Algorithm Invariants, over BFS, width-based, and additive best-first search under text, visual, and multimodal observations (Section~\ref{sec:design}). Success on such an interface can reflect operation validity or choice among valid operations. A random-valid control, which picks uniformly among valid operations, separates the two only when its decisions can differ from the reference's, so we ask whether search choice can be measured separately from operation validity.

% Paragraph purpose: Give the identity-audit receipts with both verdicts on the same runtime, then the redesigned contract and its validated measurement with the privileged-rung boundary.
On the additive best-first cells of our enumeration contract, where an expansion submits its complete candidate set and heap serials follow the sorted candidate order, random-valid is decision-identical to the exact reference on 48/48 pairs, so success there measures operation validity rather than choice.

On the same runtime with heap serials assigned in submission order, the same check finds 19/48 divergent pairs, so the check can return either verdict. The pre-registered prediction of divergence on most pairs (more than 24/48) did not hold (Section~\ref{sec:results-identity}).

The redesigned choice-frontier contract (Figure~\ref{fig:contracts}) makes the policy select which runtime-owned frontier state to expand under a binding budget (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}).
On a fresh 12-task panel from 7 domains, the pre-registered ladder test passes. M1 orders the exact reference (0.875) above exact-$\epsilon$ selectors that choose uniformly on 25\% (0.793), 50\% (0.603), and 75\% (0.347) of decisions, and random-valid (0.021) lowest, and every adjacent paired task-clustered 95\% lower bound is positive (smallest gap $+0.082$ $[+0.051, +0.111]$).

The rungs read the privileged heap head, so the ladder validates the ordering of the measurement, not any realisable policy.
