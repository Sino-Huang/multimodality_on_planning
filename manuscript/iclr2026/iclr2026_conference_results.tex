\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

% Paragraph purpose: Orient the reader: gate-first order, separate measures, and the development-stage frame that governs every claim in the section.
This section reports the executed evidence in gate order, with success, validity, invariant compliance, invalid-operation charges, budget, and learned-to-control gain kept as separate measurements and operational detail in Appendices~\ref{app:results-gates} to~\ref{app:results-boundary}. The random-valid and exact-classical references are oracle-assisted bounds, not learned abilities, and where they saturate they bound what the comparison can measure rather than certify policy quality. Every panel is a development panel, every learned arm rests on one training seed (17) except two replication cells (Section~\ref{sec:results-boundary}), and the held-out final evaluation has not been run, so no finding here is a final comparative efficacy claim.

\subsection{Enumeration-Contract Identity Audit}
\label{sec:results-identity}

% Paragraph purpose: Report the per-decision identity audit on the enumeration contract, with its structural basis and additive-only scope, before any additive row is read.
The identity audit compares random-valid against exact reference decision by decision on every additive cell of the expanded baseline, because it fixes what the additive rows of the primary matrix measure. Under the enumeration contract the two controls are decision- and expansion-identical on 48/48 additive pairs: each expansion requires the complete candidate set, and heap serials are assigned in deterministic sorted candidate order before submissions, making frontier evolution invariant to submission order. This is a property of this controller, not additive best-first search in general (Appendix~\ref{appendix}). The BFS and BFWS cells instead register choice, with random-valid at 15/24 and 17/24 against exact at 24/24 (Table~\ref{tab:results-primary-matrix}).

\subsection{Primary Matrix: Success by Algorithm and Condition}
\label{sec:results-primary}

% Paragraph purpose: Report the BFWS development-panel contrast, the expanded-baseline totals, where learned success appears, and every scope boundary, pointing to the migrated tables.
The single positive learned-control comparison comes from the frozen 15-task text-state BFWS development panel, where one fixed-seed process-SFT run achieved 0.933 success, versus 0.467 for the oracle-assisted random-valid control, 0.0 for the pretrained base, and 1.0 for exact execution, with a paired whole-instance bootstrap 95\% interval lower bound 0.2 on the gain (10,000 resamples). This is a development-stage result from one training seed, and the held-out manifest was not accessed. On the BFS and the two additive best-first development panels random-valid saturates at 100\%, so executable behavior there does not identify learned advantage. The expanded baseline (24 whole problems from 12 domains, four algorithms, three modalities, 576 model episodes independently replayed) gives process SFT 125/288 successes, versus 240/288 for random-valid, 0/288 for the base, and 288/288 for exact execution. Learned success appears only in the additive best-first cells, which the identity audit reads as operation validity. On the BFS and BFWS rows random-valid reaches 15/24 and 17/24 against 24/24 for exact execution, so choice registers there, and process SFT scores 0/24 in every modality. Against random-valid, process SFT wins zero of the 72 problem-modality units in any algorithm column. These are tiny-subgroup descriptive bounds over one training seed, and the development-panel BFWS positive does not reappear on this suite (Appendices~\ref{app:results-primary} and~\ref{app:results-contrasts}, Tables~\ref{tab:results-bfws} and~\ref{tab:results-primary-matrix}).

\subsection{Menu Manipulation}
\label{sec:results-menu}

% Paragraph purpose: Report the two menu-manipulation receipts on the frozen native adapters and state what they identify, keeping the scoped finding-2/3 wording.
