\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

% Paragraph purpose: Orient the reader: gate-first order, separate measures, and the development-stage frame that governs every claim in the section.
This section reports the executed evidence in gate order. Gate receipts and frozen scopes come first. Effect sizes are interpreted only inside the scope their gates passed. Throughout, full-episode budgeted success, operation validity, Algorithm Invariant compliance, invalid-operation charges, budget usage, and learned-to-control gain are reported as separate measurements and are never combined into a single score. The random-valid and exact-classical references are oracle-assisted bounds, not learned abilities, and where they saturate they bound what the comparison can measure rather than certify policy quality. Except for two prospective replication cells (Section~\ref{sec:results-boundary}), every learned arm rests on a single training seed (17). Every panel below is a development panel. The held-out final primary evaluation has not been run, so no finding here is a final comparative efficacy claim.

% Paragraph purpose: Report the matched-modality evidence, including the separate historical v5 matrix, and state the modality claim boundary.
\paragraph{Modality.} The per-modality rows of Table~\ref{tab:results-primary-matrix} show the same qualitative profile in all three adapters: zero learned success under BFS and BFWS, learned success below the saturating controls in the additive cells. The earlier matched-modality study, a 3-problem panel on its own ledger that is never pooled with the expanded baseline, gives process-SFT success of 5/12 in text-state, 6/12 in visual-state, and 6/12 in multimodal-state, versus 10/12 for random-valid in each modality, 0/12 for the base, and 12/12 for exact execution. Two caveats bound any such claim: the unlabelled 128px rendered views can lose symbolic identities, and the static and goal pages still contain text in every modality, so semantic parity across adapters is not established.

\subsection{Failure-Mechanism Calibration}
\label{sec:results-failures}

% Paragraph purpose: Localize failures by arm class using the frozen CPU-only calibration over replay-verified episodes, with exact counts.
A frozen CPU-only calibration mined 2,910 unique replay-verified episodes across the five executed model branches and classified all 905 failures with zero unclassified. The protocol was frozen before any outcomes were computed, and the analysis is descriptive. Three arm-level profiles emerge. The base policy fails in 447 of 447 episodes, always on the first decision: 379 emit a malformed output that never reaches the operation checker, and 67 issue rejected BFS frontier operations. Learned arms fail in 407 of 906 episodes, and the dominant learned failure kind is \texttt{other\_invariant\_violation} with 229 occurrences (56.3\% of learned failures): parsed, source-correct, applicable operations that the runtime rejects because they violate the declared search invariant. The remaining learned failures are 72 malformed outputs, 56 inapplicable grounded actions, 27 effect failures, 16 schema failures, and smaller kinds. First failures cluster early, at decision indices 0, 1, and 2 with counts 137, 88, and 46. The oracle control fails 51 times, all late and all by expansion-budget exhaustion (buckets 5--8, 9--16, 17--32, and 33+ hold 9, 9, 3, and 30).

% Paragraph purpose: Connect the failure localization to the primary matrix: where learned arms fail, and what process supervision does and does not teach.
Every baseline process-SFT episode under BFS or BFWS fails (144/144). The DAgger-iteration BFS arms fail 155/162, while the learned additive best-first families fail 56 of 546 episodes (10.3\%). This pattern matches the primary matrix (Section~\ref{sec:results-primary}). Read together with the horizon profile, process supervision teaches the policy to emit well-formed, source-correct operations, but not to maintain invariant-consistent frontier intent over a full episode: the learned policy fails early on invariant grounds, whereas the oracle control, which always emits valid operations, fails only when its expansion budget runs out late in the episode.
