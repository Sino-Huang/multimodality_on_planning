\section{Search Process Policy}
\label{sec:policy}

% Paragraph purpose: Say what a Search Process Policy is and fix its episode and observation with minimal notation.
A Search Process Policy executes a declared search algorithm one typed operation at a time, and the runtime makes every one of its decisions checkable. An episode fixes $(x, A, m, B)$, a task $x$ in a deterministic grounded planning domain, a declared algorithm $A$ given as input, the observation adapter $m$, distinct from the trained adapter scored in Section~\ref{sec:results}, and a frozen budget $B$. At step $t$, $m$ renders the decision context as a text, visual, or multimodal observation $o_t$ that exposes every fact the exact reference uses. Under the enumeration contract, Search Memory (the runtime-held search state defined below) and the candidates stay textual.

% Paragraph purpose: State the ownership split and define Typed Search Operation and Search Memory.
The runtime owns all search state and every check, and the policy owns only the operations it emits. \textbf{Typed Search Operation.} The policy emits a type from $A$'s vocabulary plus every exploration-determining operand, and an emission missing one is invalid. \textbf{Search Memory.} This is bounded runtime state (frontier, visited table, BFWS novelty counters), not model state, and $o_t$ is the policy's only channel to history.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the Algorithm Invariant, one check per algorithm family, and its limit.
\textbf{Algorithm Invariant.} The runtime checks deterministic per-algorithm properties on every operation. BFS must expand in first-in, first-out (FIFO) order, and BFWS must follow novelty and goal-count priority \citep{lipovetzky2017bestfirst}. The two additive best-first variants, weighted A* \citep{hart1968formal,pohl1970heuristic} and greedy best-first search \citep{bonet2001planning}, must follow $g + 3h_{\rm add}$ and $h_{\rm add}$ order respectively under a declared tie-break. Compliance establishes neither termination, completeness, optimality, nor success.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the Trusted Search Runtime by what it never does.
\textbf{Trusted Search Runtime.} The runtime only validates, applies, persists, or rejects operations and never chooses omitted operands, reorders candidates, or repairs, so raw emissions replay to the same trace (Operation Schemas appendix, Appendix~\ref{appendix}, and Algorithm Invariant Definitions appendix).

% Paragraph purpose: Define the two decision contracts in one place and point to the schematic and the appendix table.
The runtime supports two decision contracts, which differ in what the policy chooses. Under the enumeration contract, each expansion submits its complete candidate set, and the runtime's priority heap orders it. Under the choice-frontier contract, the policy selects which runtime-owned frontier state to expand next, under a budget of twice the exact reference's expansion count. The choice-frontier contract runs the two additive best-first variants. Its observation is scene-only, with rendered scenes of the initial state and each frontier state plus goal pages, and opaque labels are the only candidate text, so no observation modality is compared under this contract (Choice-Frontier Contract appendix). Figure~\ref{fig:contracts} and Table~\ref{tab:design-contracts} (Decision Contracts appendix) compare the two contracts.

% Paragraph purpose: Define the learning objective, process supervision on trusted Search-Trace Segments, with optional DAgger.
Process supervision is token-level cross-entropy cloning of the exact reference's operation on Search-Trace Segments, bounded slices of replay-verified exact traces. Optional DAgger (dataset aggregation) \citep{ross2011dagger} adds the exact reference's corrections on visited contexts.
