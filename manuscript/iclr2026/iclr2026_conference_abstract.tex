\begin{abstract}
% Paragraph purpose: Move from the two-competence diagnosis to executable search supervision and its bounded positive comparison.
Prior work on LLM planning finds that operational reasoning over local actions improves with scale and chain-of-thought prompting, while structural enumeration of global state spaces remains comparatively flat.
Existing training-based approaches supervise the output of a search algorithm, the final plan.
We ask whether supervising the steps of a declared classical search algorithm can teach a vision-language model to execute it to completion.
The model issues each search operation (expansion, successor generation, open-list update, or goal check) to a no-repair runtime, so replaying raw emissions reproduces the executed search.
A random baseline samples valid operations at current states to measure learned control beyond mere validity.
On a 15-task text suite under width-based best-first search, process supervision teaches execution with measurable advantage over this baseline, succeeding on 14/15 tasks versus 7/15 for random-valid and 0/15 for the pretrained base, with exact execution at 15/15 (gain 0.467, paired whole-instance bootstrap 95\% interval lower bound 0.2 (10,000 resamples)).

% Paragraph purpose: Bound that positive by broader failures, observation-channel evidence, and the exact scope of training-seed replication.
Across a broader 24-task, three-modality suite the trained policy never beats random-valid (125/288 versus 240/288), succeeding only under additive best-first algorithms.
The dominant learned failure is a source-correct, applicable operation rejected by the search invariant, while the random-valid control fails late on budget exhaustion.
Observation corruption localizes the learned controller in the language channel, where masking or shuffling collapses success (up to $-0.944$), while blanking or degrading rendered states does not (at most $-0.111$).
Zero-shot transfer and reduced second-backbone replication show no reliable gains.
All results are development-stage, with a single training seed per cell except two prospective replication cells (seeds 17/29/71, descriptive $n{=}3$). The replicated headline cell (greedy best-first, multimodal) shows advantage over the base at every seed but never matches random-valid. The DAgger iteration-1 null also replicates.
The BFWS development-panel positive remains single-seed.
Within that scope, supervising search steps moves the two-competence account from observation to intervention.
Learned search control is measurable only against baselines that already act validly, and lives in the textual scaffold rather than the rendered state.
\end{abstract}
