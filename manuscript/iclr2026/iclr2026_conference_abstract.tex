\begin{abstract}
% Paragraph purpose: State the measurement finding under the enumeration contract with its receipt pair, then the audit-driven redesign and its pre-registered gate.
We train vision-language models to execute declared classical search algorithms step by step against a no-repair runtime, and ask whether such training can be measured to improve search choice.
Under the original enumeration contract, where the policy picks each operation from a grounded candidate menu, the answer is that it cannot be measured.
A per-decision identity audit finds a random-valid control and the exact classical reference decision- and expansion-identical on 48/48 additive pairs, so choice quality is unmeasurable by construction.
Menu manipulation shows what trained policies read instead: permuting the menu leaves 36/36 successes with 0/512 invalid operations, while injecting schema-valid but inapplicable distractors collapses success to 0/36 at a distractor pick-rate of 0.6.
% Evidence: outputs/native-arms/v1/identity-audit.json; issue-130-closeout.md R1 (see Results, menu manipulation and identity audit).
We therefore redesign the decision as a choice-frontier contract, in which the policy selects which runtime-owned frontier state to expand under a binding decision budget.
Its own identity audit was a pre-registered pass condition and passes with 18/18 control pairs divergent, and headroom becomes measurable (random-valid minus exact reference $-0.644$, 95\% CI $[-0.856, -0.422]$).
% Evidence: outputs/choice-frontier/v1/evaluation/{identity-audit,analysis}.json.

% Paragraph purpose: Report the first measurement and its calibration boundary, the demoted BFWS contrast, the development-stage qualifications, and the reusable audit.
In the first measurement the adapter learns to operate the contract, solving 7/18 cells with zero invalid operations ($+0.389$ $[+0.167, +0.611]$ over the pretrained base).
Choice quality beyond the named references is not established: the contrast against random-valid is $+0.033$ $[-0.033, +0.133]$, rests on a single task, and an exploratory comparator zoo places the adapter's solve-versus-budget score (.1042) below novelty-first selection (.1250) with no positive learned Pareto dominance.
This is not evidence of equivalence, since no equivalence margin was pre-registered.
Under the enumeration contract one positive contrast exists, a 15-task width-based best-first development panel (0.933 versus 0.467 for random-valid).
All results are development-stage and single-seed except two replication cells, saturating controls bound measurability rather than policy quality, and the held-out final evaluation has not been run.

The paper reports a measurement result, not planning ability, together with two cheap benchmark-hygiene checks that any grounded-menu evaluation can run: the per-decision identity test and menu manipulation.
\end{abstract}
